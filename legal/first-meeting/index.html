The first meeting.

How the Board convenes for the first time, what it can decide, the words for each motion, and what the minute has to carry. Every step names the provision that puts it there.

Drafted Unadopted · not an instrument Bylaws v2.1, which are themselves drafted Prepared August 14, 2026
What this page is

A procedure, written down so the first meeting of this Board does not have to be reconstructed afterwards from memory. It binds nobody. It cites Bylaws v2.1 throughout, and those Bylaws are drafted and not in effect, so what follows describes the procedure the cooperative intends to follow rather than one that currently governs it.

Two questions at the foot of this page should go to counsel before anyone leans on it. They are cheap to answer and neither is on the counsel memo's blocking list yet.

Three things this meeting cannot do

  • It cannot make the Bylaws effective. Schedule A carries placeholders that must be completed first, counsel supplies the effective date, and the Secretary signs. That is counsel memo item BL-04, and it is marked blocking.
  • It cannot ratify anything. Article XII reserves ratification to a two-thirds vote of the Members. No member has been admitted, so no member vote is possible.
  • It cannot admit a member. § 1.3.3 makes membership effective only on acceptance, payment for the Stock, and full execution of the agreements the Board requires, and the Membership Agreement is not executable until Schedule A is.

Saying this aloud in the room is worth more than any motion below. A meeting described afterwards as something it was not is the defect this cooperative already carries once, and the reason it carries it is that nobody wrote down what actually happened.

Before you convene
A

Who calls it § 3.10

A special meeting may be called by the President, "or, if there is no such person, by the highest officer of the Cooperative, or by at least two (2) Directors." There is no President and no officer of any kind, so two Directors is the route. The drafters anticipated exactly this state.

No statement of purpose is required, and "any and all business may be transacted at any special meeting."

B

Notice, and why you do not need it §§ 3.11, 3.10

§ 3.11 wants prior written notice to each Director at least two days before, delivered per Article X. If that was not done, two provisions carry the meeting anyway, and both are ordinary:

Waiver by appearance. "Notice may be waived by any or all of the Directors, and appearance at a meeting shall constitute a waiver of notice thereof," unless a Director attends for the express purpose of objecting that the meeting was not lawfully called.

Consent of all Directors. § 3.10's final sentence: a meeting "may be held at any time or place (including electronically) with or without notice upon the consent of all the Directors."

An invitation to an event is not notice of a Board meeting, however far in advance it went out, because it does not tell a Director that a Board meeting will be held. Rely on the two provisions above instead. They are what the instrument actually provides for.

C

Where, and who counts as present §§ 3.12, 3.13

§ 3.12: attendance by any means of communication, including electronic, counts as presence in person, provided all Directors attending can communicate with each other during the meeting. That proviso is a condition, so the minute should record that it held.

§ 3.13: a simple majority of all Directors is a quorum. Six Directors are in office, so four. A simple majority of those present carries. If quorum is lost mid-meeting, the meeting may proceed.

Hold it as a bounded meeting with a stated start and a stated adjournment, not as a conversation inside a gathering. A quorum that dissolves halfway through costs more than the few minutes doing it properly saves.

D

Somebody has to write it down § 4.5

§ 4.5 puts minute-keeping on the Secretary, and its fallback has the President designate a recorder. Neither office exists yet, which is the honest explanation for why earlier meetings of this Board have no minutes. So the first substantive motion appoints a recorder for this meeting.

Choose the person in advance, and choose one who will still be able to write at the end of it.

The meeting
1

Call to order

SayThe Board of RegenHub, LCA is called to order at [TIME] on [DATE], at [PLACE]. This special meeting is called by [NAME] and [NAME], two Directors, under Bylaws § 3.10.
2

Notice §§ 3.11, 3.10

SayNo written notice was delivered under § 3.11. Each Director present waives notice by appearance under § 3.11 and consents to the holding of this meeting under § 3.10. Any Director attending for the express purpose of objecting that this meeting was not lawfully called should say so now.

Pause. Record any objection by name. If there is none, say so, and the minute records that too.

3

Quorum §§ 3.13, 3.12

SayPresent are [NAMES]. Absent are [NAMES]. [N] of six Directors in office are present, which is a quorum under § 3.13. All present can communicate with one another.

By name, not by count. A count cannot later tell anyone who was in the room.

4

Appoint a recorder § 4.5

MotionThat [NAME] act as recorder of this meeting and prepare its minute, the office of Secretary being presently unfilled.

Moved, seconded, voted. Before anything else.

5

Elect the officers §§ 4.1, 4.2, 3.8

§ 4.1: the officers "shall be elected by the Board" and are a President, a Secretary and a Treasurer. The President must be a Director (§ 4.2). The other two need not be. Any number of offices may be held by the same person, and § 3.8 permits combining Secretary and Treasurer into a single Secretary-Treasurer.

MotionThat [NAME] be elected President of the Cooperative.
MotionThat [NAME] be elected Secretary of the Cooperative.
MotionThat [NAME] be elected Treasurer of the Cooperative.

One motion and one vote per office, each recorded separately. This single item releases three blockages: the signature Schedule A is waiting on, the officer who executes the cooperative's contracts, and the office that keeps every record after this one.

6

The governing documents Art. XII · BL-04

MotionThat the Board approves the text of the Bylaws version 2.1 and of the Membership Agreement version 2.3, as published at techne.coop/legal/ on this date, as the governing documents of the Cooperative; that the Secretary is authorised to complete and execute Schedule A upon receipt from counsel of the effective date and of counsel's confirmation on the § 3.2.2 initial-director bracket; and that the Board records that no member ratification under Article XII is presently possible, no member having been admitted.

The last clause is the one to read slowly. It is what stops this meeting being described later as a ratification it could not have been.

7

Standing meetings § 3.9

MotionThat the Board establishes regular meetings on [SCHEDULE], held at [PLACE] or electronically, for which no notice need be given under § 3.9.

Ten seconds of business that retires the notice question permanently. Regular meetings at an established time and place need no notice at all.

8

Committees § 3.18

MotionThat the Board establishes a Financial Systems Committee under § 3.18, appoints [NAMES] to it, and charges it with [SCOPE], effective from this date.

The committee is already described across this cooperative's published pages as a body with decision rights, including a gate inside the draft treasury policy. Nothing on the record establishes it. This motion closes the gap between what the pages say and what the record holds.

9

Who signs § 4.2 · Art. XVII

§ 4.2 makes the President the officer who "shall execute all contracts of the Cooperative," except where the Board expressly delegates execution to another officer or agent. Electing a President answers this by default; naming the instrument on the record is still worth doing.

MotionThat the President is authorised to execute, on behalf of the Cooperative, [INSTRUMENT] with [COUNTERPARTY] on the terms presented to the Board.

Article XVII applies first. Any Director with a financial self-interest in the matter other than as a Member discloses it; § 3.13 bars them from voting on it; § 17.2 keeps them in the quorum count. The disclosure and the abstention both go in the minute, by name.

10

Optional, if the Board wants them MA § 1.1 · § 3.7

MotionThat under Membership Agreement § 1.1 the Board sets [PERIOD] as the period of established relationship required before admission as a Cooperative Member, in place of approximately ninety days.
MotionThat [NAME] be appointed to the vacant seat on the Board under § 3.7, to hold office until a successor is elected and qualifies.

§ 3.7 lets the remaining Directors fill a Board vacancy by simple majority, "even if less than a quorum." One of seven authorised seats stands vacant.

11

Adjourn

MotionTo adjourn. The meeting adjourned at [TIME].
What the minute has to carry

Each item is here because a provision asks for it, not because minutes conventionally carry it.

  • Date, time, place and medium, with the § 3.12 finding that all attending Directors could communicate with one another.
  • How notice was given, or the waiver relied on and its basis.
  • Attendance by name, and the quorum determination.
  • Each motion in its own words, who moved it, the vote, and the result.
  • Every dissent and abstention, by name. § 3.16 presumes a Director assented unless the dissent or abstention is recorded in the minutes, or they objected at the outset, did not attend, or gave written objection to the chair within twenty-four hours. Without a minute, silence is a yes.
  • Any Article XVII disclosure, and how the vote was taken around it.
  • Anything filed with the minute, including any § 3.15 written consent.
  • The recorder's signature, and later the date the Board adopted the minute.

A record that omits attendance and motions is a summary. It is not a minute, and it cannot answer the questions a minute exists to answer.

After
i

The minute is written the same night

While attendance and the order of motions are still recoverable. Then circulated.

ii

The Board adopts it at the next meeting

That is the moment a draft minute stops being a draft. The Bylaws prescribe an order of business for membership meetings (§ 2.10) and none for Board meetings, so nothing currently supplies that moment. This procedure supplies it.

iii

Schedule A is executed when counsel gives the date

Counsel memo BL-04: "provide effective date … then Secretary can sign." With Schedule A executed the Membership Agreement becomes executable, and admission under § 1.3.3 becomes possible.

iv

The first admission starts a clock

Membership Agreement § 2.4 requires the Board to adopt a written Patronage Plan within ninety days of admitting its first patron member. Patron members are Cooperative Members and Coworking Members (§ 1.4), so admitting a Community Participant or an Investor Member does not start it. Admit deliberately, on a date the Board chose.

Two questions for counsel
Neither is on the counsel memo's blocking list, and both should travel with the five that are

1. Which act makes the Bylaws effective, given that there are no Members? Article XII provides that amendments are proposed by a two-thirds vote of the Board or a twenty-five percent member petition and "thereafter ratified by a two-thirds (2/3rds) vote of the Members." No member has been admitted, so no member ratification is possible. The Bylaws have never been in effect, so what is happening now is initial adoption rather than amendment, and Article XII by its terms addresses amendment. BL-04 implies counsel's own view is that execution by the Secretary is the operative act. That is worth stating rather than inferring, and it is why step 6 above is phrased as approval and authorisation.

2. Does § 3.8 confine officer elections to the annual Board meeting? § 3.8 provides that the election of officers "shall occur at each annual Board meeting," and ties that meeting to an annual membership meeting that cannot occur, there being no members. § 4.1 states the power without that tie. § 4.9 offers a third route, filling "any vacancy occurring in any office," which reads most naturally as an office that became vacant rather than one never filled. This page relies on § 4.1.
