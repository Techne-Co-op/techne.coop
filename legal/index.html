Where the cooperative stands

RegenHub, LCA is in formation. The Articles are filed with the Colorado Secretary of State, and that much is settled. The rest of this site describes a cooperative that is being built: the governing instruments are in counsel review and unexecuted, no member has been admitted, and the frameworks, schedules, and policies described here are drafted rather than adopted. Nothing on this page carries the Ratified mark, because nothing has been adopted by a member vote.

We publish in that state deliberately. A cooperative whose members arrive after every decision is already made is not one anybody co-creates, so this is the working record of a live formation, and it will keep changing.

Read it accordingly. A page describing how something works is describing how it is meant to work; it binds when an instrument is executed, a board resolution passes, or the members vote. Each document above carries its own status mark. Where a page anywhere on this site and this notice disagree, this notice is right.

Defects found in the drafts after they were published are logged openly at Corrections and open readings, including the ones this estate introduced itself. What the claims audits found and did not correct, and why, is reported at The audit remainder.

What stands between drafted and effective

Saying the instruments are unexecuted is not much use unless a reader can tell what is holding them. Three things are, and this cooperative's own memo to counsel names all three.

Schedule A is not complete. The memo carries this as blocking: the Bylaws' Schedule A contains financial placeholders that must be completed before the Bylaws are effective. The Cooperative Member share price of $100.00 and annual dues of $100.00 a year are settled. An effective date has not been supplied, and it is counsel's to give.

The Secretary now exists to sign it. The memo, written before the fact, records officer assignments as pending a board decision; the Board then elected the first President, Secretary and Treasurer at the special meeting of August 14, 2026, and the steward confirmed on the coordination record, 2026-08-17, that the August 14 record stands. So the signature Schedule A waits on now has a hand to make it, and § 4.2 gives the cooperative its default signatory in the President. This paragraph previously said no officer had been elected; that was written before the meeting and corrected 2026-08-17.

None of this is hidden work. It is three board and counsel acts, and every one of them is listed for what it is in the memo to counsel, which also carries the items counsel has not yet answered.

The first of those acts is the board's, and the procedure for taking it is written down: The first meeting, a walkthrough of how the Board convenes, what it can decide, what each motion says, and what the minute has to carry. Drafted and unadopted, like everything else here.

What turns any meeting into a minute, and a minute into the record, is written at The minute protocol: the three artifacts and their three standings, what a minute must contain and which provision asks for each item, the procedure, and a template. Also drafted and unadopted.

Layer 2 · Contract — Member agreements
The Membership Agreement below carries the same reported verbal board action as the Bylaws, with the same disagreement about its date and its effect, and the same open reading at DOC-01·08: no minute supplies the meeting.
Signing is not admission. Under § 1.1 of the Membership Agreement, Cooperative Member admission is by invitation; the Board will not admit an applicant without first establishing a relationship of approximately ninety (90) days, or such other period as the Board may set by resolution; and the cooperative does not admit anyone until the Stock Price, or an acceptable promissory note, has been received. No such resolution shortening the period has been passed. A signature begins that process; it does not complete it, and nobody has been admitted under this agreement yet.

One dated obligation follows admission. Section 2.4 requires the Board to adopt a written Patronage Plan within ninety (90) days of the Cooperative's admission of its first patron member. Patron members are Cooperative Members and Coworking Members (Bylaws § 1.4), so admitting a Community Participant or an Investor Member does not start that clock. The counting rules and the labor schedule that plan depends on are drafted and unadopted, and are listed on the patronage page with what each still waits on.

The Horizon · How the cooperative could grow
Everything above is an instrument, or a reading of one. What follows is neither. The maturity model is research prepared for member deliberation: a description of how the cooperative's legal shape could grow if the work outgrows a single container, and of what would have to stay true through any such growth. It is not adopted, it is not a governing document, and it is not legal or tax advice. Nothing on these two pages binds anyone. Were the model ever to take effect, it would do so only by member vote under the amendment procedures of the Bylaws, on advice of counsel.
