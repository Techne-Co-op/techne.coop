Governance · GOV v0.1

A module specification, drafted for review: the machinery by which this cooperative's bodies act, and by which a minute becomes a projection of acts rather than an account of a room. It reads a governance model supplied by the steward, which is a drawing and carries no authority, and it states what a specification would have to decide before anything is built. Every unresolved question in the model is carried forward as an unresolved question here, classified rather than answered. Nothing in this document is adopted, and the bed it proposes has not been entered in the ledger.

GOV §1

Purpose and posture

This module would give the cooperative's own governance a body in the record: the modes a body is in, the instruments it may reach, the acts it may take, and the minute those acts project into. It follows the intake route the commons already has, the one PATRONAGE, TREASURY, AGY, and STANDING each took: a module document, a board adoption, and a graft into the ledger.2 This document is the first of those three and none of the others.

The posture is unchanged and load-bearing here in a way it is not everywhere else. Nou drafts; people decide. A module that describes how a board acts must never be a module by which a board acts, and §11 states that as a schema fact rather than a promise.

GOV §2

The model, and what standing it has

The scaffolding is governance-model-v4, supplied by the steward from the formation drive on 2026-08-14 and marked on its own face as drafted outside the agency rail.1 Outside the rail means it carries no Direction and no recorded authority: it is a drawing of a design, and this document treats it as one. Nothing in it is adopted, and reading it carefully does not adopt it.

When this module was drafted, the model file was not in this repository, and every figure here that exceeded the estate's own reading was reported as a count from the issue and marked unverifiable. That gap is closed. The steward supplied governance-model-v4 on 2026-08-17, and it is now committed at /commons/governance/model-v4/, byte for byte as received, so that a later reader can check this document against its own source.8 The counts reported from the issue were right: the model carries twenty-two acts, twenty-four event kinds, eleven invariants, and twelve anchors, and it also carries two modes with three transitions, seven instruments across four hands, five seats, and an eight-state motion lifecycle with ten edges. All of those were counted from the file's own declarations rather than from its prose.

What the reading held was a subset, and in three places it was a subset that had drifted. The estate's rendering carried seventeen of the twenty-two acts, five of the eleven invariants, and six anchors of which only four are anchors the model actually ships.3 Sections 5, 8 and 10 now carry the model's full set, and the places where the reading and the source disagree are stated in each section rather than quietly corrected. The module remains a draft. Verifying a count is not adopting a design, and GOV-D2 through GOV-D8 stay staged; GOV-D1 is discharged by the act of supply and is marked so in §12.

GOV §3

The two modes and the three edges

A body is in one of two conditions and spends almost all of its life in the first. Between the meetings, the legal state is set and no act moves an instrument. In session, the legal state is open, but only for what the body meeting actually holds. Three edges join them: convene, adjourn, and the compound one where the body adopts the minutes of the meeting just closed and stands down.3

The shape worth preserving is that each mode carries an explicit list of what it refuses, and a refusal names whose hand the act belongs to rather than simply failing. A member of this cooperative who is told no should learn from the refusal who could say yes. That is a specification requirement, not a copy preference, and it is why §5 renders the matrix as acts against seats rather than as a permission list.

edgefromtowhat it requires
convenebetween the meetingsin sessionQuorum standing at the moment it is asked for, counted from attendance, never stored (Bylaws § 3.13)

adopt and stand downin sessionbetween the meetingsA human act on a drafted minute. The Bylaws supply this moment for membership meetings (§ 2.10) and not for board meetings, which is the gap §9 carries

Against the source: the third edge is a loop, not a return. The model declares three transitions and the first two match this table exactly. The third does not: it runs from between the meetings back to between the meetings, and both acts it is built from, adopting the minutes and standing the body down, are declared as between-the-meetings acts whose precondition is that the meeting has already adjourned.8 Adjourning is what returns the body; adopting and standing down happen after it is back. The row above reads as though the minute were adopted from inside the session, and the correction matters to the schema, because it decides whether an adoption event can carry a meeting that is still open.

GOV §4

The instruments and the hands that hold them

The model's central discipline is that every instrument is held by exactly one hand, and a body reaches only its own. The estate already renders this state, instrument by instrument, with the mark each carries today.3 This module does not restate that table; it cites it, and it adds the one observation the specification needs: every instrument in the members' hand is presently unreachable, because no member has been admitted. A governance module that can only describe a working cooperative cannot describe this one in the year it is being formed, and §9 makes that the first of the four disagreements.

One derived case is worth naming here because it constrains the schema. The quorum rule is reached by nothing directly: it moves when the Bylaws move, and it is counted from who is present each time it is asked for. So quorum is a tally and never a column, in the same way and for the same reason that the substrate stores no balance.4

The five below complete the twenty-two. Each is given as the model declares it, with the seats and the mode taken from the file rather than inferred.8

Four rows in the first table say something the model does not, and the difference in each case is about whose hand holds the act. They are left standing and stated here, because which of the two is right is a bylaws question and not a drafting one.

1. Three acts the module gives to the body, the model gives to one seat. Adopt the minutes is the secretary's alone in the model, not the body's by human act; table and adjourn are the chair's alone, not the body's. The module's phrasing is the more conservative reading of the Bylaws and the model's is the more mechanical one, and the two cannot both be built. This bites GOV-D6 directly, since secretary is an office the Bylaws create and chair is not.

2. Convene is the chair's, by name. The module says those the Bylaws let call the meeting. The model says the chair and refuses any other seat. The Bylaws are the authority here and the model is a drawing, so the row above is not corrected, but a specification cannot carry both.

3. Two acts the module puts between the meetings are unrestricted in the model. Marking your own presence and noticing a motion are declared in both modes in the model; the table above places them between the meetings. The model's reading is the plausible one, since a member arriving late marks presence in session, and it is stated here rather than silently applied.

4. Amend refuses one thing, not the other. The module gives the refusal as once the question is put; the model's declared refusal is a second amendment while one stands, with the one-at-a-time rule carried as an invariant. Both are probably true and only one is written down in each place.

Two refusals belong to neither mode and to every act: a body may not switch which body is meeting, and a body may not reach an instrument the other body holds. Both are structural, and §8 asks that both become checks rather than sentences.

GOV §6

The motion lifecycle

Noticed, moved, seconded, discussion, ballot, settled, with withdrawn and tabled as branches that settle rather than erase.1 The discipline is the one the estate already applies to contributions and to the event log: a correction is a further act citing the earlier one, never a mutation of it.4 The substrate enforces that today for events, and the invariant suite proves it by attempting an update and a delete and requiring both to be refused.5

Discussion is a state, not a content. It has a beginning and an end and nothing inside it. That is the whole of §8's first invariant, and it is why a recording has nowhere to be filed: not a rule against transcripts, an absence of any place to put one.

GOV §7

The event vocabulary against the substrate

The substrate is closer than it looks and further than it sounds. events, agreements, gatherings, sessions, and attendance all exist and are applied. No motion, ballot, quorum, or minute vocabulary exists anywhere in the migration chain, in this repository's substrate or in its later migrations; that was checked rather than assumed.4 6 So this is new construction landing on tables that exist, not a retrofit.

Three facts about those tables shape the specification more than any preference could:

Event kinds are free text. The kind column is text not null with examples in its comment, not an enum. A governance vocabulary costs no schema change to introduce, which means the vocabulary is a documentation and validation decision rather than a migration decision, and it can go wrong quietly. GOV-D3 asks for the register that prevents that.

Corrections already have a home. corrects uuid references events(id) is in the applied substrate, and prior jsonb holds the prior value where a governed record changed. The no-delete discipline the model asks for is not a thing to build; it is a thing to use.

Attendance is not attached to a meeting. attendance references sessions, which references gatherings, and its comment cites the members' meeting provision. A board meeting is not a gathering in the sense that table means, so quorum cannot be tallied from it without a decision about whether a board meeting becomes a gathering row, gets its own object, or is carried as events alone. That is GOV-D4, and it is the one schema question in the set.

What a minute is, in this shape, follows: not a row, but a read over the events of one meeting, rendered in order, which stays a draft until an adoption event concerning it stands in the record. Whether that adoption is an agreement.adopted shaped event or wants its own kind is part of GOV-D3 and is deliberately not settled here.

GOV §8

The invariants, and which of them become checks

An invariant that is only prose is unaudited. The model holds eleven, which the source confirms, and the estate's reading carried five of them.8 The five are stated first, as impossibilities with what it would take to make each mechanical. The six the reading did not carry follow, in the model's own terms and without a proposed check, because proposing one is specification work this module has not done for them.

The other six, as the model states them:8

invariantwhat it makes impossible
one member, one standing castAccumulating votes by casting repeatedly. A further cast supersedes the previous one rather than adding to it
the tally equals the sum of castsWriting a settlement whose numbers do not add up. The count is derived from the casts, never stored beside them

Against the source: two of these are already checks by another name. A body reaches only its own instruments and the switch-of-body refusal are the two structural refusals §5 closes on, so this section asked for them before it knew they were invariants. One member, one standing cast and the tally equals the sum of casts are the same discipline the no-stored-balance assertion already enforces on the treasury side, pointed at ballots; and human adoption in the table above is stated by the model as the secretary's act specifically, not as a person's generically, which is the §5 disagreement arriving here from a second direction.

GOV §9

Four places where the model and this cooperative do not agree

These are the specification's real work. Each is specific, each is checkable, and none of them is resolved by drafting harder.

1. The model cannot draw a cooperative with no members. The Articles, the Bylaws, and the Membership Agreement all sit in the members' hand, and nobody has been admitted. Every act in that hand is unreachable, so the model as drawn describes a cooperative this one is not yet. The module must be able to render the actual state: zero members, six directors, one vacant seat, instruments drafted and unexecuted.3 Board-only-first, with the members' half specified and unwired, is the honest scope, and it is GOV-D2.

2. Assembled-only refuses the act that would make the Bylaws effective. The path counsel describes is that counsel supplies an effective date and the Secretary signs Schedule A. A signature is not an assembled-body act. The model gestures at this as a filing problem, an obligation that outlives the meeting, but the difficulty is upstream of filing: the record has no notion of execution or attestation distinct from adoption, and it needs one before it can describe anything this cooperative does this year.3 GOV-D5.

3. Seats and offices are different sets. The model's hands are members, board, steward, and derived. The Bylaws create a President, a Secretary, and a Treasurer, each with duties attached to particular instruments, and the Secretary's signature is load-bearing. Steward is not an office in the Bylaws at all; it is a delegation holder. Which vocabulary an act cites is undecided, and an act that cites the wrong one is unenforceable against the instrument that actually assigns the duty. GOV-D6.

4. The threshold question is already live one level up. The model asks whether a majority means a majority of those present or of the casts actually made. The estate has the board-side version of exactly this open in the corrections log: the Bylaws say a simple majority of all Directors, and whether that counts against the authorised number or against those in office is latent at six directors and live at five.7 The module should not invent an answer. It should make the question a first-class object, which is what the anchors in §10 already are.

GOV §10

The anchors register

The model ships its own unanswered questions, classified by how unanswered they are, and that is the property that makes it adoptable in pieces. Carried here with the same standing the corrections log has: an open reading is a record, not a defect to be tidied away. The model ships twelve, which the source confirms, in three classes: seven open questions, four not yet specified, and one absent.8 The six below were carried from the estate's reading, and reading them against the source produced the sharpest correction in this document: they are not six of the model's twelve. Four of them are, under other names. Seats are not offices is not an anchor in the model at all; it is this module's own observation, promoted to the register by the reading, and it belongs to §9 rather than here. Execution has no shape is broader than the model's nearest anchor, which asks only how an obligation that outlives the meeting, such as a filing with the State, can be represented. Both are kept, marked as this module's, and eight anchors the reading did not carry are added after them.

GOV §11

What this module refuses to build

R1 · No path by which an agent moves an instrument. Not this module, not an automation, not Nou. Stated as a schema fact and proven as one: the acts of §5 require a human actor, and a probe attempting each of them with a non-human actor must be refused. This is the governance twin of the treasury rule that no instrument, agent, or automation holds a key or initiates a movement.

R2 · No storage of speech. No recording, transcript, or summary enters the record as a governance artifact, and no column exists that one could be put in. If the cooperative retains recordings, they are evidence held elsewhere under a retention rule the board sets, and the surface that shows one shows its standing beside it.

R3 · No retroactive minute. The module never manufactures a minute for a meeting that had none. Meetings with no minute are displayed as gaps, and only the body can close one by adopting a minute. The June 2026 board actions are on that list on day one.7

R4 · No promotion by inference. A draft minute never becomes an adopted one by the passage of time, by nobody objecting, or by any derived condition. Adoption is an event with a human actor or it has not happened.

Decisions staged, none taken

GOV-D1 · the model into the repository · the steward · discharged
Bring governance-model-v4 into the estate so that the twenty-two acts, twenty-four kinds, eleven invariants, and twelve anchors can be checked against their source rather than reported from an issue. Done. The steward supplied the file on 2026-08-17 and it is committed at /commons/governance/model-v4/.8 §5, §8, and §10 now carry the model's full sets, and what the estate's reading had got wrong is stated in each of them. This card stays on the page because a discharged decision is a record, not an absence. The remaining seven are untouched by it: knowing what the model says decides nothing about what this cooperative adopts.
GOV-D2 · scope · the board
Does the module govern both bodies from day one, or the board only until members exist? Board-only-first is the honest answer given §9's first disagreement, with the members' half specified and unwired. The module observes this and does not decide it.
GOV-D3 · the event vocabulary · the board, on a specification
Which governance kinds enter the record, what payload each carries, which are derived rather than stored, and whether adoption of a minute is an agreement.adopted shaped event or wants its own kind. Kinds are free text in the substrate, so this decision has no migration to stop it and needs a register instead.
GOV-D4 · what a meeting is, in the schema · the board, schema category
Whether a board meeting becomes a gatherings row so that existing attendance and quorum tallying reach it, gets its own object, or is carried as events alone. This is the one question in the set that is schema rather than vocabulary, which makes it stop-and-ask under the build protocol whatever its answer.
GOV-D5 · execution as distinct from adoption · the board, with counsel
The record needs a notion of execution or attestation that is not an assembled-body act, because the act that makes the Bylaws effective is a signature. Counsel's view governs what it may be called and what it must carry.
GOV-D6 · seats against offices · the board
Which vocabulary an act cites, and how a seat maps to the offices the Bylaws create. Steward is a delegation holder and not an office, and the mapping has to say so.
GOV-D7 · visibility · the board
The model's three values, open, tally only, and held substance, against this estate's row-level policies, plus the model's own note that there are no grades of audience and no rule for when a hold expires. Bylaws § 18.1 makes this the board's call. The policies follow the decision; they do not anticipate it.
GOV-D8 · the bed and its addresses · the steward, with the Almanac
The issue suggests a GOV bed with pieces G-01 and up, and notes that G is already the gates bed. Nothing has been entered in the ledger by this document, deliberately: minting an address is a ledger act and this module has no authority to take one. A prefix that does not collide, and the entry itself, are the steward's.
