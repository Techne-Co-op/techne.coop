A Year in Formation

The story of how a room full of people who shared a lease became a cooperative that shares a record. Told by the steward, at the launch gathering of RegenHub, LCA, with every date drawn from the record itself.

dates · from the record meeting log · awaiting the steward's hand drafted by Nou from the steward's voice notes · 2026-07-27
i · the honest beginning

We organized around the least exciting thing we had

The formation began with a practical ask: Kevin held the lease and the insurance for the third floor of 1515 Walnut inside an LLC of his own, and he wanted a container that could hold them on its own feet. A self-sustaining entity, so the space would not depend on any one person's balance sheet.

That meant the first organizing variables were the lowest compounding ones: the lease payment and the expenses. Not the most exciting reason to form anything. But it was the true reason, and it sat inside a larger collection of reasons, the ones this telling is for. A cooperative formed around rent can honestly say what it is; a cooperative formed around a vision statement has to prove it. We chose to start from the ground and earn the vision.

iii · the role that emerged

Kevin asked me about my ikigai

Ikigai, as I hold it: a Japanese pattern for asking what your felt purpose and experience in life are leading you toward, and how to apply your full self to that. A pattern for self-reflective, and maybe prosocial, human development.

Kevin's question did real work. It let me craft the steward's role so that it deeply intersects my own ground: an information technologist who has often found it hard to articulate what he does, why it matters, who it serves, and what it might unlock.

The answer, it turned out, was to build the thing that does the articulating. A system whose whole purpose is legibility: what is true here, who decided it, what it is worth, and where any of it came from.

The system I could never quite describe about my own work is the one I built so the cooperative would never have that problem.the steward, first partially compensated member of the cooperative
iv · five months of building

The Commons, counted

What we call the Commons, formally the Common Information System, is the cooperative's standard record keeping: the book of record and the interface through which the cooperative operates itself. It was built in the open, one packet at a time, under ten principles that fit on a single page. Its shape makes a ladder:

Balances are computed, never stored. Every change is an event with an actor and a time. Every figure traces to the policy that produced it, and every table has an export path, so the record belongs to the cooperative and not to any vendor, including its builders. Another cooperative could take everything published and start their own; that is a design goal, not a leak.

v · two initiatives, one composition

An orchestra, not a soloist

Two initiatives live at the hub now, and the record knows them as its two principal Programs.

Program 01

RegenHub

The container for the co-working space itself: the third floor, the lease, the expenses, the daily practice of a shared physical place. The ground the whole formation stands on.

Program 02

Techne

The same placemaking intentionality carried into virtual space. A co-op of many crafts: a return to the art, the craft, and the science of practice, and to bringing intention to the tools, systems, and technologies of practice.

The bet underneath both is composition. Fields coordinated with one another compound, the way an orchestra compounds: no instrument group sounds complete on its own, and the composition makes something greater than the parts. The Commons exists so that composition can be seen, valued, and shared honestly, which is what lets it keep happening.
