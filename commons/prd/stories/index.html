Techne · the commons · product register

The participation stories

Every way a person can act in the commons and the intranet, written as capability sentences: what someone can do, how it is proven, and what its standing honestly is. One set per persona, walking the participation index from the door inward, with refusals counted as capabilities, because what the system will not do is half of what it promises. Each arrival class opens with its day one passage: what a person actually does in a first sitting, in the order they would do it, and where the sequence stops.1

The frame, named before it is used

The register is the PRD's: a capability is a member sentence, and each story below carries its proving act in the manner of a piece's acceptance.2 Personas follow the classes of the participation index, extended by the guild, the cooperative's emergent virtual way in; the rehearsal walks the newcomer, the host, and the steward in CI, with the unsigned stranger probed across every table; class-scoped personas are not among them yet, and this set is written so the guild and community personas can join when P-03 lands.3 Story numbers here are document-local for conversation, not Almanac addresses; only the Almanac mints addresses.

Each of the five arrival classes opens with a day one passage before its stories: plain narrative, first sitting, in order, and then the stop, which is the sentence naming where the sequence refuses and what it waits on. The passage claims nothing the stories below do not carry, and where a step is broken today rather than merely unbuilt, it says so in the same voice. A register that narrates only intentions is a brochure.

What this frame leaves out, deliberately: the hub's physical life (doors, desks, keys are not the record's to promise), governance meetings and voting (held under the Bylaws, not yet carried by any surface), and Class Four, which has neither a surface nor, on the legal shelf, an instrument: the Investor Agreement is still to be drafted, and a class the record cannot yet bind is a class this register will not narrate. Left out too is anything marked proposed, which stands before the board and binds nobody. Section B stands there entire: the guild waits on the board's adoption of GUILD v0.1 and on two elections, GUILD-D2, the invitation count, and GUILD-D5, the public name. Statuses shown are as of August 2026 and follow the Almanac; where a story waits, the chip names what it waits on.

A · The stranger at the door

No account, no class, no obligations. The system's first promises are made to people it does not know.

Day one

A stranger arrives holding nothing and can read everything that would bind them: the participation index, the whole legal shelf, and the commons pages, all of it rendering without a sign-in (A-1). If they decide, they apply at the front door in one sitting, with no account, no meeting, and nobody's help, and the application, the membership state, and the event land as a single act (A-2). Within moments the steward hears it on the notices rail (A-3).

Where it stops: nothing comes back to them. The acknowledgment to the applicant's own inbox waits on the mail seam clearing DNS (A-4), so from where the applicant stands, the first thing the cooperative does is go quiet. The gap is named in the steward's notice rather than swallowed, which is honest, and it is still silence.

B · The guild participant · Class Three, virtual

The guild is the cooperative's emergent virtual extension: the workshop reaching past one address to hold emergent projects at the intersection of art, craft, and science, in service of the public benefit the Articles name. An open tent with a deliberate starting edge, technologist and technology-adjacent art and craft, because that is where infrastructure and architecture bound by economic democracy get built first. What follows is that purpose expressed as machinery. Every story in it is proposed: it stands before the board with GUILD v0.1 and binds nobody until adoption, and it is written here so the promise and the machinery can be reviewed as one thing.4

Day one, on adoption

A person anywhere reads the Guild Participation Terms in plain language and signs them inline, the signature landing as an event (B-2, waiting on P-01). They choose a monthly amount they can carry, from ten to two hundred dollars, and complete enrollment in one sitting, with no surface anywhere distinguishing them by what they chose (B-1, waiting on P-02). They land in the directory holding a profile only they control (B-3), browse gatherings and register wherever a host stands a virtual door (B-4), and put the first piece of work on the board the same day, as play, practice, or work (B-5), where any bound participant can respond to them (B-6). Over the months that follow they can see their own path as a self-facing tally, never a ranking (B-7, waiting on P-04).

Where it stops, and this one is worse than waiting: the participation index today invites a reader to join the guild and hands them to a way the front door does not carry, so the very first step of this sequence refuses without saying so, on the live site. Until the board adopts and P-02 builds the fifth way, the honest guild day one is that it cannot begin. Naming that is this register's job; the remedy is either the fifth way or withdrawing the invitation until it exists.

A second thing to see before adoption: every commons policy keys on an active membership, not on a class. So what a guild participant reads on the day they enroll turns on how enrollment writes their membership and on the class question P-03 holds open, which is the same open cell B-10 declines to guess. The commons from day one is a policy decision nobody has recorded yet, and no amount of drafting here records it.

H · Sharing a project, class by class

A project is the day one word for a body of work someone brings: a thing with a shape, a person behind it, and no standing yet. The record holds no object by that name and never will, which is a decision and not an omission. This section says who can do what about a project today, class by class, without inventing one.5

Day one

Someone with a project in hand does the same first thing at every class that can do it at all: they post it to the board as play, practice, or work, say what they are looking for, and let people respond. Nothing is designated, nothing is named a project in the database, and nothing about their standing changes. If the work goes somewhere, standing arrives later and by adoption, never by the act of posting.

Where it stops: today only a cooperative member can do it, because the board is scoped to an active membership. The guild's version of this act is the piece of the guild people will feel first, and it is proposed (B-5). The carrier act, which is how a person's name attaches to a body of work, is proposed too (E-9). So on the estate as it stands, sharing a project is a member's capability and nobody else's.

H-1
A project is a body of work before the record knows it, and nothing at the origin confers standing: no amount of posting, responding, or resolving makes a body of work something the record will vouch for. Only an adopted instrument does that.
Proven by honest absence at the origin, held as a refusal in the standing module: the board and the unaffiliated shelf are the origin's only surfaces, both already built, and no piece may make a project a precondition for posting to the board.
