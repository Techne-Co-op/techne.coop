Techne · the commons · product register

The participation stories

Every way a person can act in the commons and the intranet, written as capability sentences: what someone can do, how it is proven, and what its standing honestly is. One set per persona, walking the participation index from the door inward, with refusals counted as capabilities, because what the system will not do is half of what it promises.1

The frame, named before it is used

The register is the PRD's: a capability is a member sentence, and each story below carries its proving act in the manner of a piece's acceptance.2 Personas follow the classes of the participation index, extended by the proposed guild levels; the rehearsal walks the newcomer, the host, and the steward in CI, with the unsigned stranger probed across every table; class-scoped personas are not among them yet, and this set is written so the guild and community personas can join when P-03 lands.3 Story numbers here are document-local for conversation, not Almanac addresses; only the Almanac mints addresses.

What this frame leaves out, deliberately: the hub's physical life (doors, desks, keys are not the record's to promise), governance meetings and voting (held under the Bylaws, not yet carried by any surface), Class Four (anticipated, no surface), and anything marked proposed, which stands before the board and binds nobody. Statuses shown are as of August 2026 and follow the Almanac; where a story waits, the chip names what it waits on.

A · The stranger at the door

No account, no class, no obligations. The system's first promises are made to people it does not know.

A-1
A stranger can read what the cooperative is, what participation costs, and every instrument that would bind them, before giving anyone their name.
Proven when the participation index, the legal shelf, and the commons pages render without sign-in.
Live/participation · /legal · /commons
A-2
A stranger can apply through the front door in one sitting, without an account, a meeting, or help, and the record opens with their application.
Proven when the application, the membership state, and the event land as one act for a person holding no permissions at all.
Live/commons/join
A-3
When an application lands, the steward hears within moments, and if any notice cannot be delivered, the gap is named rather than swallowed.
Proven when the intake notice arrives on the rail and a missing sink appears by name in the steward's notice.
Livenotices rail
A-4
An applicant receives an acknowledgment to their own inbox.
Proven when the email sink clears its seam; until then the absence is a named gap, never a silent one.
Anticipated · DNSnotices rail
A-5
A signed-in stranger is nobody in particular until a steward binds them, and sees what nobody sees.
Proven when an authenticated, unbound session is refused by every member-scoped read, in the words of the rule that refuses.
Live · refusalbinding · RLS
A-6
A stranger cannot see members' profiles, responses, or the intranet, because the rows never leave the database.
Proven when the probe matrix asserts every such refusal against its Authority Map cell, on every change.
Live · refusalprobe matrix

B · The guild participant · Class Three, virtual

Every story in this section is proposed: it stands before the board with GUILD v0.1 and binds nobody until adoption. Written here so the machinery and the promise can be reviewed as one thing.4

B-1
A person anywhere can join the guild online in minutes, at a monthly amount they choose from ten to two hundred dollars, and every amount opens the same doors.
Proven when the front door completes the guild way end to end in one sitting, and no surface anywhere distinguishes participants by amount.
Proposed · GUILD adoption/commons/join?way=guild
B-2
Before being bound, they can read the Guild Participation Terms in plain language and sign inline, and the signature is an event.
Proven when the terms stand on the shelf, version and standing marked, and signing lands atomically.
Proposed · terms adoption/commons/agreements
B-3
From day one they hold a profile in the directory that they alone control.
Proven when their edits land, and nobody else's do, per the profile policies already proven for members.
Proposed · on adoption/commons/directory
B-4
They can browse gatherings, register for sessions, cancel, and return, wherever a host stands a virtual door.
Proven when registration and cancellation land as events and the host's attendance record counts them.
Proposed · on adoption/commons/gatherings
B-5
They can author on the board from day one, play, practice, or work, and close what they open, saying what it led to.
Proven when a guild participant's post, resolution, and withdrawal each land as events under the board's lifecycle.
Proposed · on adoption/commons/opportunities
B-6
They can respond to any open opportunity, and the response is visible to exactly the author, themselves, and the overseers.
Proven when the probe matrix asserts the response scope for the guild persona.
Proposed · on adoptionthe board
B-7
They can see their own path as a tally: days established, invitations standing, and what remains, with every figure tracing to the events that made it. The tally is self-facing and never comparative; eligibility is a condition, not a target, which is how this story and X-3 hold together.
Proven when the path tally shown equals the tally computed by hand from the record.
Proposed · GUILD P-04path tally
B-8
They can cancel on reasonable notice, and their history remains in the record, correctable but never erased, exportable with everything else.
Proven when cancellation lands as an event and the walkaway export carries their rows like any other.
Proposed · on adoptionthe record
B-9
They cannot vote, accrue no patronage allocation, hold no hub space, are never converted to membership automatically, and their dues are never capital, at any amount.
Proven when every one of these refusals is asserted in the rehearsal for the guild persona, and the terms say the capital sentence in plain words.
Proposed · refusal setterms · probe matrix
B-10
Whether a guild participant reads the Programs view and other intranet surfaces is an Authority Map cell that does not yet exist, and this document refuses to guess it.
Proven when the cell is written with its Bylaws anchor and the probe asserts whichever answer the organizers give.

Once bound, they hold the same commons capabilities the guild stories name: a profile, gatherings, and the board.
Proven when a community persona joins the rehearsal (P-03) and walks B-3 through B-6 with the same assertions; the machinery is live for members, but no class-scoped persona has walked it.
Drafted · P-03 personathe commons

Proven by its honest absence: no surface implies the record controls the space.

C-4

Proven when the refusals hold in the rehearsal for the community persona, which arrives with P-03; until then the class-scoped assertion has not run.

X · Every participant, whatever the class

The promises that do not vary by persona, which is why they come last and bind everything above.

X-1
Nothing about you is a stored label: your standing is computed from what happened, corrections stay visible beside their mistakes, and history is not editable, for anyone.
Proven when the append-only trigger refuses every update and delete, and every tally traces.
Livethe record
X-2
Every right a page claims can be traced to the instrument that grants it, and every permission the database enforces carries its Bylaws anchor.
Proven when a policy without an anchor cannot merge and the anchors re-verify at the proof.

X-3
No score, rank, or leaderboard represents you anywhere, at any class or any amount, and no measure of participation is made into a target.
Proven when no surface renders a comparative figure of persons, and the guard holds in review; the path tally of B-7 stays self-facing, a condition shown to its own person, never a ranking.
