§1

The floor

No disbursement of the cooperative's funds on fewer than two distinct human acts: one proposal and at least one approval, and the proposer and any approver are never the same person. Declining is as recorded as approving; a refusal names its grounds. The system prepares completely and a person executes at the bank; no instrument, agent, or automation holds a key or initiates a movement (TREASURY §7, Law X).

This instrument gives the Financial Systems Committee operative work: the band 4 flagging function above, and the keep-or-strike of §5. No board act has established that committee. Bylaws §3.18 makes establishment a board act, and a committee holds only those powers and duties the board establishes, so the FSC's own existence is pending a resolution under §3.18. Every routing to the FSC in this document, here and below, should be read conditionally: if and when the committee is established, and to the board directly until then. Naming a body does not create it, exactly as §5 says of Programs.

§4

Standing purposes

Pre-authorized purposes, inside band 1 limits: a dues refund; a documented expense reimbursement against a receipt; a tax-covering distribution recorded under an adopted Allocation Run (PATRONAGE §11), which discharges a commitment already governed by its own sequence. Everything not on this list escalates to its band by amount, and anything novel in kind escalates to the board regardless of amount.

§5

The principal Programs

The cooperative's activity organizes under Programs, the organizing bodies of PATRONAGE §4, and two stand as principal. RegenHub.xyz is the workspace operations Program: the cooperatively run workspace at 1515 Walnut, its coworking rhythms, day passes, and event operations, the activity the Articles name first. techne.coop is the craft guild Program: the network of self-organizing communities of practice, its courses, gatherings, and network services. Other Programs follow by the same acts; these two are named so the FSC sizes the bands and the standing purposes with the real flows in view.

Three provisions draw the program joint through this policy. First, a money.received whose purpose is a program payment carries the Program agent in its payload, and its book partition follows Law IX: member capital facts ride the entity book, grant-bounded program flows ride the host book, exactly as PATRONAGE pinned it. Second, funding bounds are not this instrument's to set: each Program's POLICY-PATRONAGE instrument carries its own bounds (PATRONAGE §7), and a disbursement drawing on a Program's bound adds that Program's Coordinator of record to its approver trail, a proposed provision the FSC may strike. Third, naming designates nothing: a Program stands when its agent enters by recorded act and binds when its policy adopts by board act; this section names intent, not standing.

§6

The giving pool, proposed

COUNTING-RULES v2 §3 admits an election. A member may have an admissible engagement counted, valued and credited to a capital account, or given, recorded as a Recognition that carries no value, no rate basis, no delta, and no claim on any distribution. That instrument establishes the act of giving and the record of it, and stops there: it says in terms that the giving pool is a treasury matter and not a counting one, and leaves it to this instrument and to the board. This section takes up that deferral. It proposes and it does not establish. No giving pool exists, and none will until a board resolution names one.

One thing should be said before the questions, because the whole design turns on it. A Recognition moves no money. It carries no capital-account delta in either book, and it is not a receipt: nothing arrives at the bank when a member gives an afternoon. Given time creates no money, and so a Recognition does not fund the giving pool and cannot be drawn against. What can fund a pool is cash, whether given by a member, given by someone who is not a member, or set aside by the board out of a surplus the cooperative actually holds. Where a member contributes cash and elects given, the money is real and the claim on it is what the giver relinquished: the cooperative holds the dollars and the giver holds no capital credit for them. That is the one case where the election and the pool touch, and it is worth stating so that no one reads a wall of Recognitions as a balance.

§7

Standing charges: credit is not payment

COUNTING-RULES v2 §4 admits continuous cross-program work, stewardship and officer service among it, through a standing charge: a board resolution naming the body of work, its holder, the Labor Schedule tier that values an hour of it, a confirmer who is never the holder, and a term. Where a charge is in force, HOURS is admissible against it with no Opportunity required. That is a counting act, and this instrument states its treasury consequence, which is none.

An hour counted under a standing charge is a patronage credit. It posts as a valued Contribution with both deltas and it sits in the holder's capital account. It is not a payable, it creates no disbursement obligation on the cooperative, and it entitles its holder to nothing from the treasury on any timetable. What a capital account eventually reaches is an allocation under an adopted Allocation Run and whatever distribution the board then declares, which is a different sequence with its own authority, already governed by PATRONAGE §11 and by §4 above. No hour, however confirmed, moves money by itself.

If the board wishes to pay a charge holder, that is a disbursement and it comes through this policy rather than through patronage. The drafter's cut is band 4, a recorded board resolution at any amount, on the same §4 ground: a payment to an officer or to the steward for their service is novel in kind, and it is the case where the proposer, the approver, and the beneficiary are most likely to be near one another. The board should also expect a question that is not a treasury question at all. Paying a member for labor raises the character of the payment, wages against a distribution against a guaranteed payment, and with it withholding, the tax treatment of the recipient, and any conflict-of-interest process the Bylaws require for a transaction with a director or officer. That belongs to the board with counsel before the first such resolution, not after it.

One consequence follows and is proposed plainly: an hour paid is not an hour counted. Where the board resolves to pay for work under a charge, the hours that payment covers do not also post as Contributions, and where hours have posted, a later payment for the same hours would be a correction question rather than a second act. A member may be recognized for work, or credited for it, or paid for it, and the record should show which one happened.

the line this section exists to hold

Recognition, credit, and payment are three different things and the instruments should never let them blur. A Recognition records that work happened and prices nothing. A patronage credit prices the work and pays nothing. A disbursement pays, and it is the only one of the three that touches the bank. Each has its own authority: the contributor elects the first, the confirming hand of COUNTING-RULES §5 attests the second, and a board resolution under §3 authorizes the third. Nothing in COUNTING-RULES v2 obliges this treasury to move a dollar, and nothing in this section values an hour.
