The Five Minute Briefing

§1

What this is about

about 30 seconds

Say it plainly:

RegenHub has money coming in and money going out, and it has almost no adopted rules about how that money moves. That is the honest starting point. What follows is where the money actually sits today, what the machine can and cannot do with it, what we have written down, and the four things only this board can settle.

Nothing here asks you to decide anything today. It is a status report for the record.

§2

Where the money actually is

about 45 seconds

The cooperative's money lives in four places.

Stripe takes payments in. Mercury is the bank account. Xero is the set of books, the accounting record. And a Safe holds a small amount onchain.

Since the fourth of August, a computer here has been able to read all four. Read is the whole of it. Every one of those connections is read-only, deliberately. The system can tell you what the balance is. It cannot move a dollar of it.

That is the most important sentence in this briefing, so it is worth repeating in the plainest terms: no software we run can spend the cooperative's money. Every payment is made by a person, by hand, at the bank. That is a design decision, not a limitation we are waiting to remove.

§3

What anyone can see

about 40 seconds

Once a month the system reads those four places and writes a statement. Three of those exist so far, for May, June, and July.

Members see totals. They do not see each other. No names, no member identifiers, no per-person amounts, and where a group is small enough that a total would identify someone, the total is withheld. That is the privacy floor, and it holds by construction rather than by anyone's good manners.

Call those statements provisional. A statement becomes trustworthy when a human has checked it against the bank line by line and signed off on the month. Nobody has done that yet for any month. One bank feed in Xero, the account ending in one two five three, is still unreconciled.

§4

What is decided, and what only looks decided

about 50 seconds

This is where it matters that a secretary is reading it.

Two documents were adopted in July, and what was adopted was their shape, not their numbers. The board accepted how the treasury and the contribution system are built. Every threshold, every rate, every dollar figure inside them is still the board's to adopt, and none of it has been.

So when you hear that the cooperative has a spending policy: it does not. It has a draft.

The draft proposes a floor, which is that every payment out takes two different people. One proposes it, another approves it, and they can never be the same person. Above that floor it proposes four tiers by size. Small routine payments, up to two hundred and fifty dollars, take one approver. Up to twenty-five hundred, one approver. Up to ten thousand, two. Above ten thousand, the board itself passes a resolution and then the treasurer executes it.

Those numbers are a drafter's proposal. Not one of them is in force.

§5

How contributions get counted

about 45 seconds

The second draft is about counting what members contribute. It recognizes six kinds and no others: hours worked, a share of revenue, cash, delivered output, showing up, and property.

Two rules matter to this room. First, no contribution counts because someone says so. It is declared by the contributor and confirmed by a different person. Second, the rules only ever count forward from the day they are adopted, never backward.

There is a consequence in that second rule worth saying out loud. Every hour any of us has worked since February is real, and under these rules it is uncounted. That is not an oversight; it is what prospective means. If the board wants formation work recognized, that is a separate decision and it has not been made.

§6

The one hole

about 35 seconds

Hours are supposed to be valued at a tier rate, from a document called the Labor Schedule.

That document does not exist. The rates are understood to have been settled in June, and they are not in the record anywhere. Nothing but somebody's memory can tell us what they are.

Until they are written down and adopted, no hour can be honestly confirmed, because there is no rate to confirm it against. The steward's direction is to benchmark against public wage data rather than try to reconstruct the June conversation. That is the single largest gap in the money system.

§7

The money itself

about 15 seconds

As of today: thirty-eight invoices are outstanding, totaling eleven thousand ninety-five dollars and seventy-five cents. Of that, six thousand forty-seven dollars and fifty-one cents has been owed for more than sixty days.

That is real money and it is aging.

§8

What we need from the board

about 45 seconds

Four things, and only this board can do them.

One, adopt the treasury policy, which puts the two-person rule and the tiers into force.

Two, adopt the counting rules, which starts the clock on counting contributions.

Three, settle the labor rates, so hours can be counted at all.

Four, answer whether a member's share can be redeemed, and on what terms. That question is blocking a whole section of the build.

There is also a clock. The treasury committee has ninety days from the first member signing up to land four documents. Two are drafted, one is the hole described a minute ago, and the fourth, the schedule for payouts and cash-outs, has not been started.

§9

Where the detail lives

about 20 seconds

Everything said here is written up at greater length, with the citations, at techne dot coop, under commons, treasury, handbook. A two-page version sits beside it. This briefing is the spoken form and carries no authority the underlying documents do not already have.
