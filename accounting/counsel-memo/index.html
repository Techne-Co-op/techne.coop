The cooperative's accounting and tax posture: an indexed intake of questions for counsel

RegenHub, LCA is a Colorado limited cooperative association, filed February 6, 2026 under the Colorado Uniform Limited Cooperative Association Act, operating the Techne venture studio in Boulder. The book of account is Xero (financial year ending December 31); banking is at Mercury; member payments arrive by Stripe; and part of the treasury is held on-chain at a Safe multisig. The cooperative runs a common information system that records governed movements as events, with a Treasury module defining reconciliation, statements, and the discipline counsel's work will meet.

One piece of history frames several items below: before formation, the space operated under a sponsor organization's accounting, and the LCA was formed precisely to take that accounting and liability onto its own books. The seam of that shift, what carries, at what basis, from what date, runs through the entity questions in Part I and the custody questions in Part II.

Items are tagged by materiality. Blocking must be resolved before the first close or filing it touches; Advisable should be settled within the fiscal year; Clarifying is confirmatory and low-risk. Items the cooperative's own record already routes are cited to their governing documents, chiefly the Treasury module's open items table and the legal memo's tax-flagged entries.

Part I Entity and Tax Posture
AC-01 · formation record; Bylaws v2.1; legal memo tax-flagged items
Entity Classification and the First-Year Filing Calendar Blocking

The governing documents are drafted in Subchapter K vocabulary throughout: distributive share, capital account, allocation, with the legal memo flagging section 704(b) questions to tax counsel. The working assumption is partnership taxation by default classification, but no election, analysis, or filing calendar stands confirmed in the record.

AsksConfirm federal classification and whether any election is needed or advisable; confirm the first tax year (a short year from February 6, 2026, on a calendar-year end per the book); lay out the federal and Colorado filing calendar (partnership return, member schedules, state equivalents, and any local obligations); and state whether Subchapter T cooperative treatment was considered and set aside, so the record carries the reasoning and not just the vocabulary.
AC-02 · the formation boundary, 2026-02-06; sponsor-era accounting
The Opening Balance Sheet and the Sponsor Boundary Blocking

Activity before formation ran under the sponsor organization. The LCA's books need a defensible opening: which assets and obligations carry onto the cooperative's balance sheet, at what basis, effective what date, and under what characterization (contribution, purchase, assumption, or something else that the transfer documents should then say plainly).

AsksAdvise the opening balance sheet date and contents; the characterization and documentation of anything transferred from the sponsor; and the treatment of any pre-formation activity that touches 2026 filings.
Part II The Books as Found
AC-03 · TR §9, §14 (the position shown); IM Law IX (book partition)
Custody Scoping: Which Accounts Are the Cooperative's Blocking

The Xero organisation currently carries three bank-type accounts: the cooperative's own operating account at Mercury; an account connected from the sponsor organization's banking; and the on-chain treasury at a Safe multisig. As found, the book's cash total therefore includes custody that is arguably not the LCA's, and the cooperative's live bank rail reports a materially different figure than the book.

The cooperative's information model already partitions entity and host books; counsel's scoping decision should land as configuration in both Xero and that partition, so the statements the members see cite a cash line that means one thing.

AsksDetermine which accounts belong on the LCA's books; whether the sponsor-era account should be disconnected, archived with history, or carried under an explicit partition; and what the book's cash line should include going forward.
AC-04 · the Safe multisig on the books; digital asset guidance
Digital Asset Holdings: Presentation, Measurement, Character Advisable

Part of the treasury is held on-chain, predominantly dollar-pegged stablecoins at a Safe multisig, presently represented in the book as a bank-type account. Current FASB guidance moves qualifying crypto assets to fair value; whether and how it reaches stablecoins, and whether bank-account presentation is the right shape at all, deserves a settled answer before a close relies on it.

AsksAdvise presentation and measurement for the on-chain holdings; the tax character of on-chain movements (including any disposition events in stablecoin conversions); and any disclosure practice appropriate at this scale.
AC-05 · TR §8 (reconciliation as a fold), §9a (closed and provisional months)
Feeds, Cadence, and the Close Advisable

Bank feeds into the book presently lag the rails. The Treasury module defines reconciliation as three-way pairing over attested windows, and the member-facing statements distinguish closed months from provisional ones by that attestation. The bookkeeping cadence and the attestation cadence should be the same cadence, or the two definitions of "closed" will drift apart.

AsksRecommend a monthly close process and calendar the attestation can cite; confirm the division of labor between the cooperative's record (cash-basis, event-derived) and the book (accrual authority), which the statements design already assumes.
Part III Cooperative Economics
AC-06 · TR §5 (intake purposes), §14 (tax character, routed to CPA); the dues schedule instrument
Tax Character of Member Intake Blocking

Money enters the cooperative as dues, share purchases, cash capital contributions, and program payments, and the front door prices only from instruments in force. Each purpose needs its character settled: income versus contribution to capital, recognition timing, and the plain-language story a member should be told about their own payment.

AsksDetermine the character and timing of each intake purpose. The dues schedule instrument, which activates both intake pricing and the receivables view, waits in part on this determination.
AC-07 · Bylaws §5 and the legal memo's 704(b) flags; the counting rules, staged; the $250 to $500 buy-in with labor equivalent
Capital Accounts, the Labor-Equivalent Buy-In, and Section 704(b) Blocking

The buy-in is fulfillable through labor equivalent; capital accounts follow Subchapter K maintenance; and the counting rules that value member contributions are staged for board adoption. Two exposures deserve a squared answer before the first labor-equivalent share issues: services contributed for a capital interest can be compensation income to the contributing member, and the allocation method must satisfy section 704(b)'s determinism requirements, a point the legal memo already flags to tax counsel from the bylaws side.

AsksAdvise the member-side and cooperative-side treatment of the labor-equivalent buy-in; confirm capital account maintenance mechanics against the counting rules before the board adopts them; and coordinate with the bylaws-side 704(b) items so one answer covers both documents. This is the heaviest item in the memo.
AC-08 · T-05, open on redeemability; Bylaws redemption provisions
Redemption: Accounting and Member Treatment Advisable

The share redemption path is held open in the build pending a board and counsel decision on redeemability itself. When that resolves, the first redemption should not be the occasion for discovering its accounting: basis recovery, any gain or loss, and the cooperative-side entries need a settled shape first.

AsksWhen redeemability resolves, provide the redemption's accounting and the member-side treatment, including the promissory-note redemption forms the bylaws permit.
AC-09 · PP §5, §11 (commitments; the allocation run); TR §4 (settlement by pairing)
Distributive Shares: Reporting Against the Two-Step Record Advisable

The record separates allocation from movement by design: a distributive share posts as an event when allocated, and cash discharges the commitment later, possibly in a different period. Member reporting must be correct against that two-step, allocation now, cash later, including in years where they land on opposite sides of December 31.

AsksConfirm member schedule mechanics against allocation-versus-distribution timing, and advise any estimated-tax guidance the cooperative should give members alongside their first allocations.
Part IV Programs, Grants, and the Host Book
AC-10 · IM Law IX (the book partition); TR §14 (grant-bounded flows, routed per grant)
Grant-Bounded Flows and Unrelated Business Income Advisable

Program money can ride a host book partition distinct from member capital, and the record keeps the two apart by law of the information model. Per grant, the questions are funder conditions, any unrelated-business-income exposure where flows touch exempt counterparties, and the reporting that the partition should be configured to support from the first dollar.

AsksAs each grant or program engagement approaches, review its conditions against the partition design. The record routes this per grant rather than in general, and this memo will accumulate the specific instances.
AC-11 · TR §9a (the calendar-year assumption); the book's configured year end
The Fiscal Calendar, Confirmed and Recorded Clarifying

The book of account carries a December 31 year end, and the statements surface assumes the calendar year until the board records otherwise. The assumption and the configuration agree; what remains is confirmation that a calendar year is right for a February-formed partnership-taxed entity, so the board can record it and the assumption can retire.

AsksConfirm the calendar fiscal year, or advise otherwise before the first annual close.
