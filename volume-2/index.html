Its standing

Not a governing instrument. It binds nothing. What binds is the legal record; what a word means now is the Lexicon.

Volume One closed the day the articles were filed. This one opens six months later, after a launch and two nights in a cabin. Same vocabulary, tested and in places contradicted.

Held as a lens: was it agreed, or merely said?

What is different in this volume

Volume One named four absences. Three are answered here.

There are people in it. Where the tape identifies a speaker, this book says who; where it does not, it says that instead of guessing.

There is disagreement in it. Entries are marked agreed, declined, or contested. Four record an argument that never resolved, and they are the most useful pages here.

There are refusals in it. What a body declines describes it as precisely as what it adopts.

The words are first-hand. Every line set apart is verbatim from a transcript, with its sitting and timestamp.

The new absence.Some lines that entered the vocabulary this weekend were not said by a person at all. They are marked, and the instrument's rule applies: machine-made material enters as a lead and becomes the record only when a named person attests to it. Nothing here has been attested.

How to read an entry

A monospace provenance line (sitting · date · timestamp · speaker) walks any claim back to the tape. Blockquotes are verbatim. Descends from names the entry's ancestor in Volume One. The bolded label at the foot carries its standing: Open, Declined, Contested, Unresolved, or Machine-authored · lead.

Movement I

Ground

The first volume asked what kind of thing the cooperative is and answered soil. This one puts that answer under load. Soil now has a lease, three officers, and a decision to make about what it may hold — and in one five-minute conversation it acquires the verb it had been missing.

Tending

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 01:19 · the president, then the secretary

The soil metaphor finally gets a verb, and it arrives as a correction to itself. Soil is a substrate — a noun, a condition, something that sits there being fertile. Tending is what someone does to it. The whole exchange is four words long and it settles a year of argument about whether the cooperative is passive infrastructure or an active party.

We're like tending to an ecosystem, in which this wider community of practice can connect. recorded · verbatim · sitting seven, 01:19 — the secretary's entire reply: it's tending.

It is also the only word in the vocabulary that names labour rather than substrate. That makes it the term most directly owed to the counting rules, which do not yet exist: tending is work, and the ledger cannot presently see it.

One whole, not many entities

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 00:00 · the president

The structural question the first volume left open — the dual entity strategy, the deferred second cooperative, the portfolio of ventures each finding its own vessel — is answered here in one sentence, and the answer is: not yet, and maybe not at all.

These things could become their own entities, their own cooperatives, whatever. But I'm of the opinion that we should run the cooperative as one whole. recorded · verbatim · sitting seven, 00:00

One body running a coworking space, a cafe, and a technology research lab, with the lab as the key framing. What keeps this from being a land grab is the clause the secretary adds immediately after it, which is the next entry.

The maturity model that lets work leave

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 00:42 · the secretary

Emergent work is held inside the cooperative as a project, with a stated path by which it can become its own thing if it needs to. The phrase used is as if it is soil-like — soil that can release what grew in it, rather than soil that keeps everything it fed.

This is the difference between a commons and a holding company, and it is one clause long. Without it, running as one whole means the cooperative accumulates every venture it touches. With it, the cooperative is the stage a venture passes through.

The cooperative does not own the community of practice

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 01:25 · the president

The most consequential rule proposed across the whole weekend, because it decides what the cooperative is for. The community of practice is not an asset of the co-op, not a membership funnel, and not a thing to be claimed.

We're not trying to own or claim that community of practice. But we are committed to creating a context and conditions for that community of practice to collaborate effectively. recorded · verbatim · sitting seven, 01:25

Stated from the other side a moment later: being in the practice does not make you a member of the cooperative. That is the first volume's concentric circles restated as a duty rather than a diagram — the co-op tends the outer rings without any expectation that they travel inward.

Soil as the end of an argument

sitting six · 16 Aug · 05:21 · the president

The metaphor's other use, and the one worth watching. A detailed proposal for pooling compute is met with three escalating objections on a morning walk, and the third one is not about the proposal at all.

We're the soil. Our responsibility is to create conditions for these things to thrive. It's not going to benefit us to be doing all this nitty-gritty of selling compute to members and the credits. recorded · verbatim · sitting six, 05:21

Soil is a generous idea and it is also an unanswerable one. It resolves arguments by changing what kind of thing is being argued about. The first volume noted that the metaphor dissolved an entire class of accounting anxiety in one move; this sitting shows the same move dissolving a specific proposal, and the proposer accepting it without conceding the idea.

Contested.Not resolved. Within the hour the same two people built four accounting documents together with no objection from either side. The disagreement was never about structure as such — see Which structure earns its overhead, Movement III.

The membrane

sitting one · 14 Aug · 12:04 · the secretary

An unusual thing to say while trying to recruit: that membership is first a demand. It asks something of you, it carries tax consequences, and the boundary around it is held deliberately rather than by default.

Becoming a cooperative member asks of you. It's a very conscious membrane that we're holding around the membership. recorded · verbatim · sitting one, 12:04

The corollary, said publicly two hours later, is what makes it honest: the public benefits do not require membership. You can be served by the cooperative without joining it, which is what stops a conscious membrane from being a velvet rope.

Trust is the soil

sitting two · 14 Aug · 21:40 · an engineer, from the floor

The best fusion of the two vocabularies in the building, and it came from the technical side rather than the ecological one. Platforms entered a field of existing trust and harvested it without tending, and what they left is a social dust bowl.

Trust is the soil our community grows in. The networks came into this field and harvested — and harvested, and paid no attention to the tending practices. We're now living in a social dust bowl. recorded · verbatim · sitting two, 21:40

The lesson drawn from it in the same breath is a discipline rather than a lament: the commune did not change agriculture, the organic label did. What made regenerative practice spread was making it legible to a market, and the same is being asked of regenerative networks.

Movement II

Recognition

The first volume's longest argument was how to see a contribution without pricing it. Six months on, the argument has a deadline and a new participant. The deadline is a patronage plan owed within ninety days of the first member. The new participant is a machine that can propose what counts, which makes the question of who witnesses a claim as important as the question of who makes it.

Counting rules

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 03:14 · the secretary

The formation record's central unresolved problem, finally given a shape precise enough to be written. Counting rules name what is a gift and therefore left off the ledger, and what is work and therefore stays on it — intersecting cooperative intellectual property, pay, compensation, and labour. They name what is patronage-worthy.

If we recognise multi-capital exchange, including gifts and care work — giving together, saving together, investing together — the counting rules name what is a gift and left off of a ledger, what is work and remains a part of a ledger. recorded · verbatim · sitting seven, 03:14

Note what the definition concedes. Deciding what is a gift is deciding what the ledger cannot see, which means the generous half of the definition is also the invisible half. Every warm word in both volumes — care work, tending, community energy, culture-setting — is waiting on this one document to learn whether it is a virtue or an entry.

The witness line

sitting three · 16 Aug · 38:17 · the secretary

The rule that governs every instrument the cooperative builds, and the reason this book can be read at all. An instrument stands between a record too large to hold and the person trying to read it. It transduces; it does not decide. Anything it produces enters as a lead, and becomes the record only when a named person attests to it.

An instrument stands between the record and the reader, the transducer. It turns the whole into something a person can take in, and every figure walks back to its source. recorded · verbatim · sitting three, 38:17

The cost is stated in the same passage and is worth keeping: a record built this way will always look incomplete, because it always is. Completeness is a reading, not a target.

Attestation, arriving twice

sittings three and five · 16 Aug · 11:24, then 02:10 · a visiting builder, then the room

The same word enters the weekend from two directions and is never reconciled. In the afternoon it is cryptographic: one party vouching for another with a stated confidence, recorded as its own entry on a graph. Late that night it is social, and half a joke: we actually do need to have our agents start attesting to each other.

Between those two senses sits the whole design question of the year. A vouch is cheap and computable; a witnessing is slow and accountable. The cooperative has adopted the word without yet choosing which one it means.

One voice, late.The night line was said in a loose register, in a room where several people were falling asleep. It is kept because it is the only place the two senses touch.

The trust graph, and the question under it

sitting three · 16 Aug · 00:49, 14:18 · a visiting builder; a guest

Trust made computable, carefully plural: there is no single graph, and every community computes its own result from its own data and its own rules. The secretary's addition is that the qualitative half is not a rounding error — recognising each other's intelligence is itself a dimension of trust, which makes the whole thing curation rather than control.

Then a guest asks the question that stays open for the rest of the weekend.

Everything there is someone's identity. Do you need to know everyone's identity to work with your system? recorded · verbatim · sitting three, 14:18

The answer offered is that the parties settle it among themselves within a gradient of privacy, security and access. That is a reasonable thing to say in a demonstration and an insufficient thing to build on: a trust system whose privacy model is a negotiation between parties does not yet have a privacy model.

A matrix with prices on it

Movement III

Circulation

The first volume decided not to price contributions and still had rent. This one keeps that problem and adds a clock. Somewhere in these entries the cooperative stops speculating about post-capitalist protocols and starts owing four specific documents to its own first member.

The four documents

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 02:51 to 04:20 · the secretary

The most concrete plan made across the whole weekend, stated in ninety seconds with nobody objecting. A finance and accounting committee, chartered by the incoming board, owes four documents:

  1. Counting rules — naming what is patronage-worthy.
  2. A labour schedule — benchmarking rates for technologists around Boulder and Denver.
  3. A treasury policy — naming the patterns of reconciliation and how the body holds itself to account on ledger.
  4. A payout, cash-out and reinvestment schedule.
Members can actively either claim their cash dividend or reinvest it back into the cooperative as this, like, cybernetic feedback loop. recorded · verbatim · sitting seven, 04:20

The last of the four is the first volume's clearest decision — community reinvestment over profit sharing — given a mechanism instead of a preference. And the reinvestment path is the only place in either volume where the steering metaphor attaches to actual money.

The ninety-day clock

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 03:14 · the secretary

A real deadline, and the only one in this book that is not self-imposed: within ninety days of the first member signing, the cooperative voluntarily adopts a patronage plan. Every economic idea in either volume is downstream of it.

It has not started, because no member has been admitted. The formation record ran on external deadlines and the first volume observed that this is what made it finish in six months rather than a year. This is the next such date, and it is waiting on a signature the cooperative controls.

The human hour

sitting four · 16 Aug · 08:54 · the secretary

The most ambitious economic proposal of the weekend and the one with the least support behind it. Fiat is not itself a sound unit. An hour is: it is the basis of every timetable, labour role, and labour card there has ever been. A cooperative could recognise credit hours as a unit of value carrying a dividend, a reinvestment, or a gift.

If an hour could be a new coordination medium as an alternative currency to the fiat dollar — I see the LCA being in a position to essentially recognise credit hours as a unit of value. recorded · verbatim · sitting four, 08:54

The unit is only sound until you ask whose hour, valued how — at which point it needs a labour schedule, which is document two. The hour and the benchmark rate arrive in different sittings and are the same problem.

Monetary polyculture

sitting four · 16 Aug · 04:42 · the secretary

The frame that holds the hour, the compute credit, and the grant in one family: a shift from monetary monoculture to many kinds of money, each carrying a different perception of value.

The agricultural metaphor carries its own cost honestly. Polyculture in a field is a management practice, and it is more work than a monocrop. Plural money means plural accounting — which is precisely the overhead the cooperative was told, two mornings later, that it should not take on yet.

The compute pool, declined

sitting six · 16 Aug · 00:05 to 06:09 · the secretary; the president

A complete design delivered on a walk in under five minutes. If the investor buy-in is five thousand dollars, break a unit into fifty shares of a hundred, so participation starts at a hundred; add a ten to twenty percent operational allocation for cooperative labour; allocate the capacity by a timesharing credit borrowed from tourism, housing cooperatives, and community land trusts.

Service delivery as a unit that can be broken down into compute, bandwidth, and memory — and functions similar to a postage stamp in its ability to guarantee a level of fulfilment. recorded · verbatim · sitting six, 00:05

It is turned down on the spot, twice as a judgement about the design and once as a judgement about the designer. It is entered here in full because a book that records only what a body adopted describes it half as well as one that records what it declined and why.

Descends fromCommunity data trust (Meetings 7, 14); The information right (Meeting 14)
Declined.Not adopted, not withdrawn. Rejected as premature rather than wrong: maybe something like that can emerge over time, but trying to force that right now, we'd be jumping way too far ahead of ourselves.

Which structure earns its overhead

sittings six and seven · 16 Aug · 06:09, then 02:51 · the president; both

The weekend's most quoted line is a personal one, and it looks like a verdict on temperament.

I still think that you have a tendency to overly structure things. There's a way that we're emerging in a shared giving economy that doesn't actually need to have a ledger for every single share that happens. recorded · verbatim · sitting six, 06:09

Read with the next sitting in place, it is not that. Within the hour the same two people build a great deal of structure together — four treasury documents, a maturity model, a rule about what the cooperative may and may not own — and neither objects to any of it. So the disagreement was never structure against emergence. It was about which structure earns its overhead now: a market for pooled compute, no; the documents a cooperative legally owes its first member, yes.

Contested, then narrowed.Kept as a tension because both parties still hold their premise. What changed is its scope.

Give-first, and the ledger it keeps anyway

sittings five and six · 16 Aug · 11:40; 06:20 · the secretary; the president

The counterweight to the whole accounting programme, and it has one working instance in this book. On the Friday night a member hosts the group's model capacity from his own subscription, states his usage aloud, and sets his own ceiling: he will stop at seventy-five percent of the week. A pooled resource, governed by a stated rule, with no ledger.

The general form is the president's: trust the relationality of the web without overly structuring every relationship. The honest qualifier is that a giving economy still keeps accounts — in memory and in reputation, unevenly distributed and with no appeal. The live choice is not whether to count but whether the count is written where it can be argued with.

Stake-backed promises

sitting three · 16 Aug · 20:32, 21:53 · a visiting builder

An agreement with capital locked behind it, resolved by a named arbiter — which might be a state account, a proof, or a council of machines with a human fallback. The capital is not spent, it is encumbered: still yours, still earning, and not withdrawable until the obligation unwinds.

The secretary's translation is not cryptographic but professional: a lawyer's trust retainer, prepaid and drawn down as a sequence of commitments is fulfilled, refined over several hundred years. The same primitive, with precedent attached.

Descends fromMutual staking (Meetings 5, 6, 10); Capital allocations instead of loans (Meeting 5)
Note the floor.An encumbrance presumes capital to encumber. A hundred-dollar participant and a five-thousand-dollar participant cannot make the same promise under this primitive, which is the first volume's sliding-scale problem in a new instrument.

Risk markets, not prediction markets

sitting four · 16 Aug · 02:46 · a visiting builder

The cleanest piece of mechanism design in the weekend. If you bet on whether a thing will fail, and you find the flaw, you are paid to keep quiet. If instead you underwrite the risk of failure, you are paid only when it does not happen — so the person who finds the flaw is paid to say so.

It is worth keeping beside Goodhart's Law from the first volume. Both are entries about what a measure does to the people measured, arrived at from opposite directions: one warns what a target corrupts, the other designs an incentive that survives being known.

Movement IV

Form

The first volume watched aspiration meet the Colorado Secretary of State. This one watches the filed entity try to act. It turns out that a cooperative becomes operational by saying particular words out loud in a particular order — and that saying them in front of an audience is a different act than saying them in a room.

Corporate incantation

sitting two · 14 Aug · 37:00 · the secretary

The weekend's best piece of legal anthropology, offered half as a joke and entirely meant. Certain sentences must be spoken during a board meeting for the act to be lawful — not because the words are magic but because their absence is evidence of a failure of due care.

You have to say things during board meetings to follow the rule, or else you could be thrown in front of a court and be accused of not showing due care. recorded · verbatim · sitting two, 37:00

The practical consequence, which the first volume's pace-layering entry anticipated: the fast layer of a cooperative is made of speech. Bylaws are amended by vote; a vote is a sentence; the sentence has to be witnessed.

Three motions, one vote

sitting two · 14 Aug · 44:11 · the president

The script called for three motions and three separate votes. The room combined them into one, and rather than tidy it away afterwards, a director named the irregularity aloud on the recording and asserted why it was still lawful.

This entry exists because of a rule the first volume set for itself and could not keep: there are no failures in a book made of summaries, because summaries record what was said rather than what was later amended. Here the amendment and the original are both on the same tape, four minutes apart.

Consequence.Two days later the treasurer's election was flagged by the secretary as a vote that probably has to be redone. It was formally reaffirmed at the next board sitting, outside this volume's window. A combined vote cost the body one procedure and gained it a public moment; both halves of that trade belong in the record.

The recording is not the record

sitting two · 14 Aug · 38:11 · the secretary

Said in the same breath as the live meeting, and it is the sentence that keeps this whole practice honest. An audio record establishes that protocol was followed. It is not the minute. The minute still has to be made.

The distinction matters more here than in most organisations, because this one is trying to run its governance through its information systems. A cooperative whose memory is excellent and whose minutes are late has built the wrong half first.

Thin and thick

sitting two · 14 Aug · 31:02 · a visiting scholar

The sharpest question anyone asked all weekend, and it came from outside the cooperative. It followed two stories: a co-op whose shared layer turned out to be almost nothing beside the culture built around it, and a century-old dairy that keeps its cooperative story underneath the story of its ice cream.

What layers do you expect your shared organisation to hold as opposed to the member organisations holding? How thick do you imagine the collective to be? recorded · verbatim · sitting two, 31:02

Instrument

sitting three · 16 Aug · 36:26 · the secretary

The unit the cooperative builds in, defined precisely for once: an instrument is a specific type of record — in practice a table — that recognises a lexicon within itself. A log is what happened; an instrument is a log with a point of view about what is worth counting.

Which is why the counting rules are not a bookkeeping chore. Choosing the columns is the political act; everything downstream is arithmetic.

Technology research lab

sitting seven · 16 Aug · 00:19 · the president

Its limits

What this book does not hold

Four absences, stated plainly, for the same reason the first volume stated its four.

It does not name everyone. The transcription assigns speaker numbers per file and the numbers do not agree between files. Where a person named themselves on the tape — into a roll call, into a consent round — this book says who. Everywhere else it says a visiting builder, a guest, the room. Some of the best lines here belong to people this book cannot credit, which is a smaller loss than the first volume's and the same kind.

It is a book of a good weekend. These three days included a launch, a retreat, and eight consenting recordings. They are not representative of an ordinary month, and a body that reads its own high points back to itself will overestimate how settled it is. Most of what a cooperative does is not said aloud into a recorder in a room full of visitors.

The machine hand is in it and not fully marked. One entry is explicitly machine-authored. But models ran through this whole weekend — generating, summarising, answering in a shared channel — and their effect on what people then said is not separable from the tape. Every entry here was also sorted, titled and framed by a machine reading the transcripts. By the cooperative's own rule that makes this a book of leads, and no entry in it has been attested by a named person.

It closes early on purpose. The record does not stop on 16 August. The tapes point forward to sittings days later carrying decisions this book cannot see — officer elections reaffirmed, a departure clarified, a foundation proposed, a sum of money under discussion. This volume is bounded at the weekend because a season should be closed before it is read, not because the story pauses there.

The working agreement of this volume

The first volume gathered what was said while a coworking space talked itself into becoming a cooperative. This one gathers what was said in the first three days it had to act like one, and the difference between the two books is mostly the difference between vocabulary and consequence. Where an entry here contradicts an entry there, neither is wrong: the term moved, and both volumes keep the afternoon it moved on.

Read as a lens, this volume asks the same question of a proposal as its predecessor, and one more. Can the thing being built be found in these pages — and was it agreed, or was it only said?
