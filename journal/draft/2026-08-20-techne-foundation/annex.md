## 1. What this is, and what it is not

This is a verbal report from a meeting. There is no vote, no resolution, no filing, no term sheet, and no announcement. The rename is Gitcoin's decision and Gitcoin's news to tell. Nothing here should be published, posted, or repeated outside the membership until Kevin confirms what he is comfortable having said in public.

Source: the LCA Board and Operations Meeting of August 19, 2026, transcript held in the cooperative's meeting archive. Speaker attribution in that transcript is partly reconstruction; only passages attributable to Kevin Owocki with confidence are used below.

## 2. What was proposed

Gitcoin is choosing a new brand within the next week or two. One shape on the table is renaming it **Techne Foundation**, and at the same time domesticating the entity — presently a Cayman or Bahamian foundation — in the United States as a 501(c)(3). It would sit beside the cooperative as a separate legal entity, with both organizations building the same name and network in their own ways.

Reasons Kevin gave:

- It makes research grants, beginning with but not limited to the first one, systematic rather than one-off.
- It lets him contract the cooperative as an organization rather than hiring members one by one.
- The precedent he is working from is Pia Mancini's Open Collective and Open Source Collective: one for-profit, one non-profit, close enough in name that they read as one body of work.
- A 501(c)(3) structure limits extraction concerns.

Two conditions he set, both of which must hold:

1. The cooperative comes back to him **unanimously**, with a yes and with our conditions.
2. He is able to convince the Gitcoin branding group.

Three things he volunteered:

- He named his own conflict: he has hired a number of our members, does not want to misuse influence over the cooperative, and asked us to come to him deliberately, as a separation of containers.
- He assessed the whole thing as **high upside, low probability**.
- He said the word carries baggage for some people, and that **one of our organizers, told over the weekend, said it was not a good idea.** That objection is on the record and its reasons have not yet been heard by the rest of us. Against a unanimity bar it is dispositive of the process, whatever anyone concludes on the merits.

## 3. The question actually in front of the board

Not whether *techne* is a good word. The question is narrower, and it is a governance question:

**Should the cooperative consent to a separate legal entity, with which it shares people and a lead investor, adopting its registered trade name?**

`techne.coop` is our registered Colorado trade name (Secretary of State, August 12, 2026, document 20268011137). `techne.institute` is also ours, and carries a published seminar. Consenting to a shared name is a decision about the cooperative's identity, its distinguishability to counterparties and to the public, and its exposure to whatever the other entity does under that name. That is a board matter regardless of how good the philosophy is.

## 4. What we get, and what we take on

**In favor**

- A funded, aligned 501(c)(3) whose remit overlaps ours, able to contract the cooperative as an entity instead of picking off members individually. That is a direct answer to a real fragility of ours, and it is the strongest item in the yes column.
- Shared brand-building compounds rather than competes.
- The research grant becomes a program rather than a favor.
- Our prior use of the name for an educational purpose is genuine and documented, which strengthens rather than weakens a shared-name arrangement — provided it is disclosed now.

**Against, or at minimum unresolved**

- **Name-sharing is one-way once done.** Consent can be withdrawn going forward; association cannot be undone retroactively.
- **Distinguishability.** Two entities, overlapping people, one name. Counterparties, funders, and regulators will conflate them. Someone will eventually ask which one they are dealing with, and the answer must already exist in writing.
- **Reputational coupling runs both directions.** Their conduct lands on our name, and ours on theirs.
- **The trademark landscape.** Eight live "Techne" marks are on the record, including in software and scientific classes. Neither entity has cleared the name. A rename amplifies whatever risk our trade name already carries.
- **A 501(c)(3) has constraints a cooperative does not**, particularly on political activity. Some of the framing in the memo may be language their counsel cannot adopt.
- **The conflict Kevin named does not dissolve because he named it.** Members who are his employees or contractors are not free agents in a unanimity vote.
- **Our own investor member class is not yet established.** A decision that ties us to an outside entity is being made before that class exists to be consulted.

## 5. Conditions, stated in full

The memo carries five in the steward's voice. Their full form:

1. **A written name-use understanding** — what each entity may say about the other, what neither may say, how consent is evidenced, and how it ends.
2. **Trademark position settled before either party builds on it** — co-ownership or a perpetual irrevocable license running to the cooperative, and clearance by counsel on both marks before either commits.
3. **A distinguishability statement** both entities publish: what Techne Foundation is, what RegenHub, LCA / Techne is, and what the relationship between them is and is not.
4. **Lane separation** — the nonprofit holds the data standard; a cooperative holds the software built around it and licenses to local instances. Separate boards, separate books, no shared officers without a disclosed and recorded decision.
5. **A disclosure and recusal protocol** for members with an economic relationship to Kevin or Gitcoin, applied to this vote and to any subsequent contracting.
6. **A divergence clause** — what becomes of the shared name if the two purposes part.
7. **Nothing published by either party about the other** until both have adopted the above.
