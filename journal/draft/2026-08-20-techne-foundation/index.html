On the name

To The board and organizers, RegenHub, LCA

From Todd Youngblood, Ventures and Operations Steward

Date August 20, 2026

Subject Why I would support renaming Gitcoin to Techne Foundation

What Kevin asked

Gitcoin may change its name, and Techne Foundation is one of the names on the table. The entity would also move to the United States as a 501(c)(3). Kevin asked whether this cooperative would back the name. I would, and here is my thinking in plain terms.

Everyone's work runs on somebody's tools

A farmer, a potter, a nurse, a researcher, a programmer: none of them can work without tools, and almost none of them own the ones that matter most. Whoever owns the tools sets the terms for everyone who uses them. That is the problem worth building an institution around. Not technology itself, which is too big and too vague to argue with, but who owns it and who decides how it works.

Techne is the old word for craft and skill, the kind you learn with your hands and your judgment. It covers the potter and the engineer equally, and so does the problem. That is why the name fits: it points at something every kind of practice shares, instead of staking out one industry.

What the foundation would actually do

Companies report themselves to the world through financial statements, a few numbers published every quarter. That tells you what a business earned. It tells you almost nothing about what was actually made, who made it, what it took, or who ended up better off. Groups that are not companies, such as cooperatives, trusts, and neighborhood projects, have no good way to report themselves at all, so they end up borrowing a corporate form that was never built to describe them.

The work is building that missing way of accounting: a plain record of what a group did, in language its own members can read, and a shared vocabulary for comparing the different ways people can own things together. Two older bodies of work give us the bones for it, one that tracks resources, events, and agents (McCarthy, 1982), and one that describes how tools and training actually extend what a person can do (Engelbart). Ostrom and the commons researchers give us the ground to stand on. That is the program, and techne is the word wide enough to hold it.

This blames no one

Concentration of ownership is an arrangement, not a conspiracy, and it outlasts everyone inside it. Blaming individuals is both unfair and useless. Aiming at who owns what lets us criticize the arrangement without accusing people, which keeps the work readable to everyone and keeps a 501(c)(3) educational rather than adversarial.

What I am asking for, and what I am not

  1. Back the name in principle, as our answer to Kevin. Not money, not staff, not our own mark.
  2. Put our conflicts on the record. We registered techne.coop as a Colorado trade name on August 12, 2026, and our cooperative expects to create an investor member class that the Board has not yet established. Both tie us to a Techne Foundation. Kevin is not a neutral party here either. Better said now than discovered later.
  3. Leave the trademark question to Kevin, who is running that search. He needs to know about our registration.

Who owns our tools, and who decides how they work, is the conversation nobody is having. I would rather we be on the record backing an institution named for the whole of craft than watch the name go somewhere smaller and be right about it later.

Todd Youngblood · Ventures and Operations Steward, RegenHub, LCA
