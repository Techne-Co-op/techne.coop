On the name

To The board and organizers, RegenHub, LCA

From Todd Youngblood, Ventures and Operations Steward

Date August 21, 2026

Subject Why I would support renaming Gitcoin to Techne Foundation

What was asked

Gitcoin is choosing a new brand. One candidate is Techne Foundation, and the entity — presently a Cayman or Bahamian foundation — would domesticate here as a 501(c)(3). Kevin asked whether we would back it, unanimously and with our conditions, by today; his second condition is his own branding group, and he puts the odds at high upside, low probability.

Three facts belong in front of the argument. Kevin named his own conflict unprompted: he has hired a number of our members and does not want to misuse influence, which is why he asked us to come to him deliberately. He wants to contract the cooperative as an entity rather than hire members one at a time, which answers a real fragility of ours and is the strongest thing in the yes column. And one of our organizers, told over the weekend, said it was not a good idea. Against a bar of unanimity that is not a footnote.

Why I would back it

Everyone's work runs on somebody's tools. A farmer, a potter, a nurse, a programmer: none can work without them, and almost none own the ones that matter. Whoever owns the tools sets the terms for everyone who uses them. That, and not technology in general, is the problem worth an institution.

Techne is the old word for craft and skill, learned with hands and judgment. It covers the potter and the engineer equally, and so does the problem. Its root means a dwelling — built, shared, owned by nobody in it — which is also how two institutions can carry one name without either owning the other.

The program is an accounting we do not have. Companies report themselves through a few quarterly numbers — in Plato's sense a drug not for memory but for reminding, supplying the opinion of knowing what an enterprise did while saying almost nothing about what was made, by whom, at what cost, or who ended up better off. Cooperatives, trusts, and neighborhood projects have no such instrument at all and borrow the corporate one, inheriting its false fluency. Building the missing record, and a vocabulary for the ways people can own things together, is work for an educational institution rather than a company. McCarthy gives us the bones, Engelbart how tools extend a person, Ostrom the ground.

This accuses no one. Concentration of ownership is an arrangement, not a conspiracy, and it outlasts everyone inside it — pleonexia, the appetite to have more than one needs, is a problem of formation, not of villains. Not softness, though: left alone the arrangement hollows out the conditions a republic runs on. Aiming at the arrangement rather than at people is accuracy, and it keeps a 501(c)(3) teaching rather than accusing.

Five things said out loud first

I am asking us to back the name in principle — not money, not staff, not our own mark — and not before these are settled:

  1. Where the trademark lives. Kevin raised this himself. Co-ownership or a perpetual irrevocable license to us, in writing, before either party builds on it. Eight live "Techne" marks are on the record; neither of us has cleared it.
  2. Lanes. Benjamin's shape: the nonprofit holds the data standard, a cooperative holds the software and licenses to local instances. Separate boards and books; no shared officers without a recorded decision.
  3. How each of us describes the other. Neither claims affiliation, origin, or funding from the other without written consent, and either may require a correction.
  4. Divergence. Open Collective's pair worked because the people stayed aligned. That is a friendship, not a structure. Say now what happens to the name if the purposes part.
  5. Our own conflicts. Members employed or contracted by Kevin are not free agents in a unanimity vote. Disclosure at minimum, recusal where the interest is direct.

Our conflicts, on the record

We hold Colorado trade names for techne.coop (August 12, 2026) and techne.institute, and the first Techne seminar — Plato on craft, with Paul Diduch of the Cosmos Institute — is published on the second. That is prior use, by us, for exactly the purpose a Techne Foundation would claim. We also expect an investor member class the Board has not established. Kevin is running the trademark search and knows none of this. He should today.

The answer I am asking for

Yes, with conditions, conveyed as our unanimous position; no, plainly and with reasons; or not on this timeline, the honest answer if unanimity is not reachable today. A rushed unanimity on sharing our name is worse than a missed window.

Todd Youngblood · Ventures and Operations Steward, RegenHub, LCA
