DRAFT. Adopted by no one. The rename is Gitcoin's news. Nothing here goes public before Kevin says it may.

On the name

To The board and organizers, RegenHub, LCA

From Todd Youngblood, Secretary and Officer

Date August 21, 2026

Subject Why I would support renaming Gitcoin to Techne Foundation

What Kevin asked

Gitcoin is picking a new name. One option is Techne Foundation. The entity is a Cayman foundation today and would move to the United States as a 501(c)(3). Kevin wants a unanimous answer from us, with our conditions, by today, and he wants his own branding group to sign off. He thinks the odds are low and the upside is high.

Three things belong up front.

  • Kevin raised his own conflict before anyone else did. He has hired several of our members and does not want to lean on that. That is why he asked us as a group.
  • He wants to contract with the cooperative as an entity, instead of hiring our members one at a time. That fixes a real weakness of ours. It is the strongest reason to say yes.
  • One of our organizers heard about this over the weekend and said it is not a good idea. When the bar is unanimity, that is not a small thing.

Why I would back it

Everyone's work depends on someone else's tools. A farmer, a potter, a nurse, a programmer — none of them can work without tools, and almost none of them own the ones that matter. Whoever owns the tools sets the terms for everyone who uses them. That is the problem worth building an institution around. Not technology in general.

Techne is an old Greek word for craft: skill learned by hand and judgment. It fits the potter and the engineer equally well, and so does the problem. Its root also means a dwelling — built, shared, owned by nobody inside it. That is a fair picture of two organizations sharing one name.

The work itself is a kind of accounting we do not have yet. Companies describe themselves with a handful of quarterly numbers. Those numbers give the feeling of knowing how a business did while saying almost nothing about what got made, by whom, at what cost, or who was better off. Cooperatives, trusts, and neighborhood projects have no measure of their own, so they borrow the corporate one and inherit its blind spots. Building that missing record is a teaching job, not a company's job.

None of this blames anyone. Concentrated ownership is an arrangement, not a plot, and it outlives everyone inside it. Aiming at the arrangement instead of at people is both more accurate and what keeps a 501(c)(3) teaching rather than accusing.

Five things to settle first

I am asking us to support the name in principle. Not money, not staff, not our own mark. And not before these are settled:

  1. Who owns the trademark. Kevin raised this himself. We want joint ownership or a permanent, irrevocable license, in writing, before either side builds on the name. Eight live "Techne" marks already exist. Neither of us has cleared them.
  2. Who does what. Benjamin's version: the nonprofit holds the data standard, a cooperative holds the software and licenses local versions. Separate boards, separate books, no shared officers without a recorded decision.
  3. How we describe each other. Neither side claims the other as an affiliate, a founder, or a funder without written permission. Either side can require a correction.
  4. What happens if we drift apart. Open Collective's two entities worked because the people stayed aligned. That is a friendship, not a structure. Decide now who keeps the name if the purposes split.
  5. Our own conflicts. Members who work for Kevin are not neutral voters in a unanimous decision. Disclose at a minimum; step aside where the interest is direct.

What we should tell Kevin today

We hold Colorado trade names for techne.coop (August 12, 2026) and techne.institute. The first Techne seminar — Plato on craft, with Paul Diduch of the Cosmos Institute — is already published on the second. That is prior use, by us, for the exact purpose a Techne Foundation would claim. We also expect an investor member class the board has not created yet. Kevin is running the trademark search right now and knows none of this. He should hear it today.

The answer I am asking for

One of three: yes, with conditions, given as our unanimous position; no, plainly and with reasons; or not on this timeline, which is the honest answer if we cannot reach unanimity today. Rushing to agree about sharing our name is worse than missing the window.

Todd Youngblood · Secretary and Officer, RegenHub, LCA
