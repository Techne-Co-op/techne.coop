Federation

A cooperative keeps a record of its own work, and sooner or later that record has to meet other people’s. This page states what federation means to this cooperative: how the record is meant to travel, what decides which part of it leaves, what we commit to, and what we invite. It is a vision, written down so nobody has to ask, and it is nobody’s obligation but ours.

What federation means here

Not a merger, and not a platform anybody joins. Federation is two bodies keeping their own records, in their own words, and being able to hand each other a defined part of what they hold, under a rule each of them wrote down first. Nothing about it requires either one to move onto the other’s system, adopt the other’s vocabulary, or give up the ability to walk away.

That reading has consequences the cooperative takes as position rather than preference. The first is that there is no single-protocol future worth planning for: the work a body does internally and the record a public reads want different properties, and one substrate serving both serves neither well. The second is that the join between two bodies is not a wire. It is shared vocabulary and shared governance, which is to say it is mostly a writing problem, and the technical part is the easy half.

One record, two rings

The cooperative keeps one record. An entry is written once, carries its provenance, and is never edited, which is what lets it travel later without losing its authority. That record stands in two rings, and there is a written rule between them.

the inner ring

Where the work happens

Coordination, approvals, the day to day, and the identities that speak for the cooperative. The full record lives here, and it belongs to the members, who are the people it is about.

Operational. Not public, and not published by default.

the outer ring

What the public may read

A defined digest of the inner record, at a stated grain, published so that anyone can read it, cite it, and hold the cooperative to it. Today the outer ring is this web estate, the pages you are reading.

Public. A projection of the record, never the source of it.

What crosses between them is decided by rule, not by judgment on the day. The Egress Doctrine is that rule: whose the record is, whose the public digest is, and a written table deciding which subset departs, at what grain, through which named door, with corrections travelling outward through the same door a claim did. The doctrine stands drafted. It binds when a person with standing adopts it, and not before.

Deny by default is the starting position. A record without an egress rule leaks by accident and hoards by default, usually both at once, and the cure for both is the same written table.

What the cooperative commits to

Four commitments, stated from this cooperative’s own record and binding on nobody else. They are the shape any federation with us would have to fit, and they are written here so that a reader can hold us to them before there is anybody to federate with.

  • The record travels with its provenance, or it does not travel. A claim that leaves this cooperative carries where it came from and how fine its citation is. A digest that cannot be traced back to an entry is a rumour with good typography.
  • Our vocabulary is published, and mapping beats merging. The words the record uses are a versioned artifact, held in agreement with the page that governs them by a check that fails when they drift. Across a boundary the deliverable is a mapping, not a merger: this term here means that term there, at this grain, with these losses. Neither body has to adopt the other’s language, and neither should be asked to.
  • Nothing leaves except by a rule written in advance. What may be said publicly about a member, a program, or a transaction is decided by an adopted doctrine and not by whatever an interface happens to expose. Where the rule is still a draft, as it is today, the answer is that nothing leaves.
  • Identity is meant to end up the person’s. The end state this shape implies is that a person carries their own identity and their own record of practice between bodies that federate, rather than holding a separate account inside each of them. An identity that can leave with the member sits closer to open and voluntary membership than any account table we would build for ourselves. It is not built, and today the account you sign in with here is the cooperative’s.

What it invites

No invitation on this page is addressed to a particular body, because none has been extended and this is not the place one would be. What the cooperative invites is narrower and more useful than a partnership: read the rule before it hardens, and tell us where it is wrong.

Three things are readable today, and all three are drafts:

· The Egress Doctrine, the rule for what leaves. One of the elections it leaves open is whether a member’s consent to being named is gathered once, standing, or per departure. That is a question for the people it would name.

· The Lexicon, the cooperative’s vocabulary as an artifact a machine can read, which is the half of federation that is cheapest to do early and the half that decides the rest.

· The Almanac, the register where every piece of this is addressed, marked, and open to being argued with. This page is entered there at X-30.

Anyone building along these lines is welcome to take the vocabulary, the doctrine, or the argument and use it. Nothing here asks for a relationship in return, and nothing here creates one.
