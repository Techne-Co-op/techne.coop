A cooperative of many crafts, and how it came to be one.

What Techne is, what it believes, how it is legally held, who tends it, and the older lineage it works inside. Everything here is on the record, and the record is public.

01What this is

A coworking space that talked itself into a cooperative.

Techne began as a room in downtown Boulder where people of different crafts worked side by side. The room worked, and the people in it started asking why: what made the work here feel different, and what would it take to own that difference together rather than rent it from anyone. Answering took fifteen formation meetings, from August 8, 2025 to February 6, 2026, and the answer was a cooperative.

On February 6, 2026 the Articles of Organization were filed with the Colorado Secretary of State, document number 20261163853. On August 14, 2026 the doors opened publicly, and the cooperative held its first board operations in front of the room. Between and around those dates runs a written record: the meetings, the drafts, the decisions, and the building of this site itself, published while it happens.

A community of practice, held in common. Soil, not a firm. The front page carries the words the room uses for itself; this page carries the plainer facts underneath them.

02What we believe

The seven cooperative principles.

The drafted Bylaws subscribe to the seven International Cooperative Alliance cooperative principles, in their recital, by name. The Bylaws are in review with cooperative counsel and are not executed, so this is a statement of what the draft holds, not of what has been adopted; the principles themselves are more than a century of cooperative practice, and they are why a cooperative rather than any other shape.

  1. Voluntary and Open Membership. Open to those able to contribute or patronize and willing to accept the responsibilities, without discrimination.
  2. Democratic Member Control. Controlled by the members, who actively set policy and make decisions.
  3. Members' Economic Participation. Benefit follows contribution and patronage rather than capital invested.
  4. Autonomy and Independence. A self-help organization controlled by its members, on terms that keep it so.
  5. Education, Training, and Information. The cooperative teaches its members and informs the public about what cooperation is.
  6. Cooperation among Cooperatives. Cooperatives strengthen one another by working together.
  7. Concern for Community. Working for the sustainable development of communities, local and beyond.

The recital's full text is in the drafted Bylaws, which remain in review and unexecuted. The summaries above are this page's paraphrase; the instrument's wording governs the instrument.

03How it is held

A limited cooperative association, for public benefit.

The legal entity is RegenHub LCA, a Colorado limited cooperative association organized as a public benefit entity. Among its stated public benefits is cultivating scenius: the collective intelligence that emerges from a community, named in the room's own vocabulary and written into the filing.

Techne is the studio name, the name of the practice this cooperative holds in common, and techne.coop is the registered trade name it works under in public. One entity, one practice, one door: the legal container and the community it holds are deliberately distinct, and the container is kept thin so the work stays with the people doing it.

The governing instruments, the Bylaws and the Membership Agreement, are drafted and in review with cooperative counsel; neither is executed, and no member has been admitted yet. The legal record holds the instruments and says plainly what is settled and what is not.

04Who tends it

Roles, held by people.

The record names offices; the people holding them are met in the room, not on this page. What the cooperative has, today, is this:

A steward of ventures and operations

The hands-on tending of the cooperative's daily work: the space, the systems, the build of this record, and the care of what grows here between decisions.

A board of directors

Seated in June 2026, when the formation-era organizers gave way to a board. The board carries the cooperative's governance until members are admitted and can carry it further.

Officers of the cooperative

Elected by the board on August 14, 2026, at the launch gathering, in front of the room: a president, a secretary, and a treasurer. The board confirmed each election by a separate motion on August 19, six to zero. Who holds each office is named on the legal shelf.

Membership is intended to open now that the doors are; until the instruments are executed, no one holds membership, and the roles above are the whole of the structure. The ways in describe how a person steps toward it.

05Lineage

Ancient meets today.

Nothing here is new, and that is the point. The cooperative works inside lineages much older than itself, and it says so.

Technē

The Greek word underneath the name: craft, art, skill, technique. Not technology in the narrowed modern sense, but the whole practice of making things well, with the knowledge that lives in the hands. The cooperative takes the old word seriously: the craft of code and the craft of neighboring are the same kind of thing, and both demand care.

The commonplace book

An old habit of readers and makers: copy out the lines worth keeping, arrange them so they can find each other, and let the collection become a mind's working record. This estate keeps the habit twice over, in the formation record of what was said then and in the operating record of what is true now.

Intelligence amplification

The mid-century thread from Vannevar Bush's memex to Douglas Engelbart's augmentation of human intellect: the conviction that the right instruments make a group of people durably smarter, and that building those instruments is a craft in its own right. The cooperative builds its tools in that spirit, for itself first, as augmentation rather than automation.

The cooperative movement

The oldest lineage of the four in organizational form: people who need the same things owning the means of them together. The seven principles above are its distilled practice, and a limited cooperative association is simply its current Colorado vessel.
