The record

Where the cooperative stands in its own governance: which mode it is in, what each instrument is and whose hand may move it, what the record can and cannot hold, and what is missing. This page is a reading of the published instruments. Nothing on it runs, nothing on it is adopted, and no act on this surface moves anything.

The two modes

A cooperative is in one of two conditions at any moment, and almost all of its life is spent in the first. Naming that plainly is the point: the ordinary condition is not a gap between the interesting parts.

Where we are

Between the meetings

The legal state is set.
Permits
  • Mark presence for a coming meeting
  • Set the order of business
  • Notice a motion
  • Record an interim act under a delegation
  • Adopt the minutes of the meeting just closed
  • Convene, once quorum stands
Refuses
  • Move, second, amend, withdraw
  • Call the question, put it, cast, settle
  • Anything that would move an instrument by delegation

In session

The legal state is open, but only what this body holds.
Permits
  • The whole floor: move, second, amend, call, put, cast, settle
  • Point of order
  • Table
  • Adjourn, never over an open ballot
Refuses
  • Recording an interim act; in session a body acts by motion
  • Switching which body is meeting
  • Reaching an instrument the other body holds

The cooperative has been between the meetings for its whole existence, because no meeting of this Board has ever been convened on the record. The procedure for the first one is written at The first meeting.

The standing state

Every instrument, the hand that may move it, and the mark it presently carries. A body reaches only its own instruments: a meeting of the Board is refused at a members' instrument, and the refusal names whose hand it is rather than simply failing.

What this record cannot hold

Stated as impossibilities rather than rules, because a rule can be broken quietly and a missing capability cannot.

A minute that reports what someone said
There is no event kind for speech. Discussion is opened, and nothing said in it enters the record. The capability is absent, not merely refused, which is why a recording or a transcript has no standing here: the record has nowhere to put it.
A record that certifies itself
The minutes are a projection. The lines write themselves from the events, and they stay a draft until a person adopts them. Adoption is human.
An act whose effect is an absence
Withdrawn and tabled are settlements, not erasures. Every motion that ever stood is still there afterwards, and a correction is a further act rather than a change to an earlier one.
A quiet change to the instruments between meetings
No delegation reaches an instrument. The legal state moves only by an assembled body, which is the governance twin of the rule that no instrument, agent, or automation holds a key to the treasury.
A stored quorum drifting from who is in the room
Quorum is a tally, never a number. It is counted from attendance each time it is asked for.
What is missing

A record that shows its holes is worth more than one that looks complete. These are the gaps as they stand tonight.

The minute book is empty.

No meeting of this Board has a minute. The June 2026 board actions are reported in four accounts that disagree on the date and on whether anything was adopted at all, and none of them is a minute: no attendance, no motions, no vote, no quorum. The open reading is logged at DOC-01·08. It closes when a director who was present states what was done, or when the board adopts the current instruments afresh on a record that has all of those things in it.

Execution has no shape here.absent

The act that makes the Bylaws effective is a signature, not a vote. A model in which the legal state moves only by an assembled body cannot describe it, and this one presently cannot. Naming it is the first piece of work.

Seats are not offices.open question

The vocabulary above speaks of hands: members, board, steward, derived. The Bylaws create a President, a Secretary and a Treasurer, each with duties attached to particular instruments, and steward is not an office in them at all. Which one an act cites is undecided.

Which majority, of what.open question

A majority of those present, or of the votes actually cast? The two differ the moment anyone abstains. The board-side version of the same question is already open: § 3.13 says a simple majority of all Directors, and whether that counts against the authorised number or against those in office is latent at six and live at five.

Nobody can answer an interim act.absent

A member who disputes something done under delegation has nowhere to say so. The ledger would be readable but not answerable.

There are no grades of audience.open question

Privacy is a single hold: substance held, the fact of the act open. Nothing distinguishes board-only from member-only from public, and no rule says when a hold expires. Bylaws § 18.1 makes that the board's call.

Presence is required to vote.not yet specified

Nothing handles a member who cannot attend. For a cooperative that intends to be distributed, that is a large silence.

Standing

Specified, not wired. This page describes a governance module for the Common Information System; the module is proposed and unbuilt, and the substrate carries no motion, ballot, quorum or minute vocabulary today. The model it follows is a drawing, drafted outside the agency rail and carrying no authority of its own. Where this page and an instrument differ, the instrument is right and this page owes a correction. Instruments are indexed at the legal shelf; defects found after publication are logged at Corrections and open readings.
