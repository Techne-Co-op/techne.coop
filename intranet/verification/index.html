The verification walk

The cooperative checks its own claims by hand, and calls that a walk. These are the same cards as the public run-book, the step-by-step guide anyone can walk out loud into a recorder. Here you click the verdict and type it instead.

What this page is

A checklist of the claims the cooperative makes about itself, one card per claim. The running count is just below. Each card names one claim, tells you where to go look, and asks you to say whether it held up. You are the one checking. Nobody has checked on your behalf.

Where the walk stands

The last sitting was the steward’s, on 2026-09-04: forty-eight verdicts, tiers one through four entire, 44 holds, 3 fails, 1 deferred. Thirty pieces took the verified mark on his sentences, and the ledger now reads 79 verified against 29 delivered, of which eight are still walkable here. What is left is the three two-person pieces, the six attestations, the coda card, and the four he could not close: U-15, U-11 and U-20 failed and are filed as U-33, U-34 and U-35 for repair, and U-19 is deferred until someone can sign out and watch the map’s absence. His write-up is archived with the walk sheets in the repository, at commons/build/verification/walks/2026-09-steward-walk-03.md.

What you can do here

  • Walk one card or all of them, in any order. Nothing here has to be finished in one sitting.
  • Do a card’s steps, then click one verdict: holds (you saw it work), fails (you saw it not work), not ready (the thing is not built yet), deferred (you could not check it today), attested (you are signing your name to a proof).
  • Type one sentence saying what you actually saw. The verdict without the sentence is not much use to anyone reading later.
  • When you are done, go to the record of this walk at the bottom, copy it, and hand it to a steward.
Your verdict is recorded; it is not yet a mark. When you click a verdict and write your sentence, both go into the cooperative’s record as your testimony, under your name, at the moment you speak them. That means the walk survives a closed tab and resumes on another machine, and it means a second person can read what you saw. It does not move anything. What moves a mark is a person taking this walk and opening a pull request, a proposed change that others review before it is accepted. Where this page and the ledger, the cooperative’s register of record, disagree, the ledger is right. A verdict with no sentence is held in this browser only, because the record will not take one: a verdict without a sentence tells the record what happened and not what was seen.
