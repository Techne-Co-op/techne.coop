# LABOR-SCHEDULE benchmarking: the evidence base

**Status: research only. Nothing here is adopted, proposed for adoption, or published.**

This directory is decision support for the treasury committee and the steward. It is
deliberately outside the page estate: no file here is served, linked from a public
page, or entered in the ledger. If any number below reaches a member, an instrument,
or a rate actually paid, it got there by a human act that is not recorded in this
directory.

## Why this exists

The labor schedule, meaning the tier names and the rates attached to them, was
adopted by reputation and never written down anywhere in the estate. A forensic
audit found that the only authority for it was a circular citation: documents citing
each other, with no originating act at the end of the chain. The steward's chosen
path, 2026-08-13, is to benchmark against public wage data rather than to reconstruct
the schedule from anyone's memory, in this sequence:

1. the shape of the schedule (tiers and what distinguishes them),
2. then the rates,
3. then confirmation by the directors.

This directory covers step 1 and assembles the evidence for step 2. It does not take
step 2 and it cannot take step 3.

## Files

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | this note |
| `shape.md` | a proposed schedule shape, tiers only, with three rate scenarios per tier |
| `sources.md` | every source, with URL and retrieval date |
| `oews-may2024-boulder-denver-colorado.csv` | the extracted federal wage rows the scenarios are computed from |

## What a reader should not do with this

- Do not treat the tier names as decided. They are a drafting proposal and the
  committee may reject the whole cut.
- Do not treat any scenario as a recommendation. Three are given precisely so that
  none reads as the answer.
- Do not cite this directory as authority for a rate paid to anyone. It is evidence
  about the outside market, not a record of a cooperative decision.
