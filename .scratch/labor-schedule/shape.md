# A proposed shape for the labor schedule, with rate scenarios

**Research only. Nothing here is adopted.** Source keys S1 through S5 refer to
`sources.md`. Read that file before quoting any number in this one.

## 1 · The shape question comes first

The steward's sequence puts shape before rates for a reason worth restating: a tier
is a claim about what the cooperative thinks distinguishes one kind of work from
another, and once rates are attached, arguments about the rate quietly become
arguments about the claim. So this section proposes tiers with no money in them at
all, and the money arrives in section 3 as three alternatives, none recommended.

The proposal cuts by **the nature of the accountability**, not by job title, seniority,
or credential. That choice is itself contestable and is listed as an open question in
section 5.

## 2 · The proposed tiers

Five tiers. Each names what the person is accountable for, and the anchor occupations
are the federal codes used in section 3 to price it, not a list of who belongs.

| tier | the work | accountable for | anchor codes (S1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 · Support and stewardship** | Keeping the place and the books running: space operations, front of house, scheduling, records, bookkeeping entry | Executing defined work reliably. Judgment about how, not about what | 43-6014, 43-3031, 25-3021 |
| **T2 · Coordination and facilitation** | Running programs, events, and member-facing processes end to end | A process and its outcome, within a scope somebody else set | 13-1121, 21-1099, 27-1024 |
| **T3 · Practice** | Professional craft applied to the cooperative's work: development, design, writing, analysis | The quality and fitness of the work product itself | 15-1255, 27-3042, 13-1111, 15-1254 |
| **T4 · Lead practice** | The same craft, plus responsibility for a body of work across people and time | A whole workstream, including what other people's work has to fit into | 15-1252, 15-1211, 13-1082 |
| **T5 · Direction** | Setting what gets done and answering for whether it was worth doing | Outcomes the cooperative is exposed to, and the calls that produced them | 11-1021, 11-9199, 11-3021 |

Three properties this cut is trying to hold:

- **One person may sit in more than one tier in one week.** A studio's economics
  depend on it. The schedule should price hours, not people, or the first person who
  does both will be mispaid in one direction or the other.
- **The tier is not a rank.** Nothing in T5 is worth more as a human contribution than
  anything in T1; the tiers price exposure and scarcity in the outside market, which
  is what benchmarking measures and all it measures.
- **Five is a guess.** Four collapses T3 and T4, which is defensible. Six splits T1.
  See section 5.

## 3 · Three rate scenarios per tier

All figures are **hourly, in 2024 or 2026 dollars as marked**, and are for
deliberation only.

### Scenario A · Floor-anchored and compressed

Sets the bottom tier at the MIT Boulder County living wage for one adult (S3,
$27.09, 2026 data), well above the City of Boulder statutory floor (S2, $16.82 in
2026), and caps the top at **2.0 times the bottom**, which is the ratio the large
majority of US worker cooperatives hold (S4). Intermediate tiers step geometrically.

| tier | rate | notes |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | $27.09 | the living wage anchor itself (S3) |
| T2 | $32.22 | |
| T3 | $38.31 | |
| T4 | $45.56 | |
| T5 | $54.18 | 2.00x T1, the S4 ratio |

What it costs: every tier from T3 up is below the Boulder market 25th percentile, so
this scenario prices the cooperative out of hiring at market for practice and
direction work, and depends on members accepting a discount for reasons other than
cash. Say that out loud if this one is chosen.

### Scenario B · Market 25th percentile

Each tier is the unweighted mean of its anchor occupations' **25th percentile hourly
wage in the Boulder, CO MSA**, May 2024 (S1).

| tier | Boulder p25 | Colorado p25 | spread to T1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | $20.28 | $19.54 | 1.00x |
| T2 | $25.26 | $23.85 | 1.25x |
| T3 | $36.95 | $38.06 | 1.82x |
| T4 | $53.25 | $46.58 | 2.63x |
| T5 | $64.90 | $59.27 | 3.20x |

What it costs: T1 at $20.28 is above both statutory floors but **below the living wage**
in S3, which is a hard thing for a cooperative to publish. And the 3.2:1 spread is
well outside the sector norm in S4.

### Scenario C · Market median

Same construction at the **median** rather than the 25th percentile.

| tier | Boulder median | Colorado median | spread to T1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | $24.30 | $23.22 | 1.00x |
| T2 | $30.35 | $29.71 | 1.25x |
| T3 | $52.73 | $49.84 | 2.17x |
| T4 | $66.97 | $58.58 | 2.76x |
| T5 | $83.90 | $76.06 | 3.45x |

What it costs: this is the honest market rate and it is the most expensive by a wide
margin. T1 is still under the living wage in S3, which is worth pausing on: it means
the market itself does not clear a living wage for support work in Boulder County, and
no benchmarking exercise can hide that. A cooperative that benchmarks to the median
and floors T1 at the living wage has taken a position, and should be able to say it is
one.

### Reading the three together

The three scenarios disagree most where it matters most: **the ratio**. A at 2.0x, B at
3.2x, C at 3.45x. The tier shape is stable across all three; only the spread moves. The
committee's real decision is probably the ratio, and the tier rates then fall out of
whichever anchor and ratio it picks. That framing is offered as an observation, not a
recommendation.

## 4 · Adjustments the committee will have to rule on

Not priced here, because each is a policy question rather than a data question.

- **Staleness.** S1 is May 2024. Boulder's statutory floor rose twice since. If a
  scenario is adopted from these figures, it should carry an explicit escalation rule
  rather than being quietly restated later at the same numbers.
- **Employee against contractor.** Every OEWS figure is an employee wage and carries
  no self-employment tax, no benefit load, and no unpaid time. A contractor rate built
  from these numbers without a multiplier is a pay cut in disguise. Hypha (S5) uses an
  explicit 1.05 contractor modifier; the true load is usually larger.
- **Remote and geography.** S5 carries explicit geographic modifiers. If the
  cooperative benchmarks to Boulder and engages people who do not live there, it needs
  a stated rule, and "we pay the Boulder rate everywhere" is a legitimate rule as long
  as it is written down.
- **Non-cash consideration.** Any labor valued into an instrument leans on the same
  rates, which is why this is not only a payroll question.

## 5 · Open questions, all of them for people

1. Is the cut by accountability right, or should it be by craft, or by seniority
   within craft? This memo picked one and can defend it, but nobody decided it.
2. Five tiers, four, or six?
3. Which anchor: living wage, market 25th, market median, or a floor from one and a
   ratio from another?
4. What maximum ratio does the cooperative hold itself to, and is it in an instrument
   or in practice?
5. Does the schedule price hours or people?
6. What escalation rule keeps it from going stale the way the last one did?

## 6 · What this does not do

This memo does not reconstruct the prior schedule, and it should not be read as
evidence about what the prior tier names or rates were. The audit finding stands: that
schedule's only authority was a circular citation, and nothing in public wage data can
retroactively supply the act that was never taken. Whatever the committee adopts is a
new schedule adopted now, and the record should say so plainly rather than presenting
it as a restatement of something already in force.
