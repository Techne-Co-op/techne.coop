what growsthe ecological grammar

Seeds, ventures, and programs.

The cooperative keeps an ecological grammar for what takes shape in it. This page names the forms. It will fill with the things themselves as they grow.

01The grammar

Three forms, told apart by hands and habitat.

The grammar already runs through the estate: the front page keeps it, and the Almanac builds by it. What tells the forms apart is not size or ambition but commitment: how many hands are on the thing, and whose care keeps it alive.

Seed

One contributor's tending

An idea planted in the common ground and tended by the person who brought it. Most things start here, and many rightly end here; a seed asks nothing of the cooperative but soil.

Venture

Two or more hands committed

A seed that has recruited care beyond its planter: two or more hands committed to it, working as one effort. A venture has its own name, its own pace, and its own relationship to the cooperative that holds the ground under it.

Program

A habitat the cooperative sustains

A living structure the whole cooperative keeps: not one project's effort but a habitat other work depends on, sustained deliberately, the way a garden keeps its beds and paths.

02The soil

What the metaphor is actually for.

The soil metaphor is the oldest piece of the cooperative's vocabulary, and it was chosen to settle a real question: is the cooperative a productive enterprise that signs contracts and delivers work, or something more like infrastructure? The answer that held was soil, the substrate that creates conditions for growth. The value appears in what gets built on top.

The metaphor did practical work the day it arrived. If the cooperative is soil, it does not need to account for every member's minute-by-minute contribution; it tracks its relationship to the ventures and projects that grow in it, and an entire class of accounting anxiety dissolved. The cooperative layer stays deliberately thin: it provides conditions for ideas and collaborations to thrive rather than asserting control conditions upon them.

The metaphor entered in Meeting 3 of the formation series, September 12, 2025, and its full account is kept in the Commonplace.

03The beds

Categories today. A garden in time.

This page is honest about its own season. Today it names the forms of growth; it does not yet present a portfolio, because presenting one is a decision about what is ready to be named in public, and that decision belongs to the people doing the work. As seeds are planted, ventures commit, and programs take root, this page will fill, each thing appearing when its makers say it is ready.
