Palette & Component Reference

Working reference for the Techne design system, updated to the v4 palette. Blue is the primary accent, the color of what a reader does; ember is the secondary, the cooperative's own mark on structure. Both are held against a warm parchment ground through dark and light modes, with a sunset range reserved for section atmosphere and data. Toggle modes with the control in the top-right; it follows your device setting by default.

State & Sunset

The previous reference carried seven status hues. The v4 palette narrows this to three earth states for condition and adds a sunset range for data, on the argument that fewer colors, each paired with a word, read more reliably than a wide status wheel.

Three earth states · condition

Green for what is operational or ratified, blue for what is in review or simulated, red for what is pending or wants attention. They appear only on instruments, never tint a panel or a heading, and each is always paired with a word and a square mark, so no condition is carried by color alone.

Sunset range · atmosphere and data

A six-stop sweep from twilight to last light, ordered by luminance so a sequential scale degrades to grayscale and reads for colorblind viewers. As atmosphere it can tint section structure; as encoding the warm five stops form a magnitude scale. It never colors a control and never carries the sign of a number.

Site Navigation · The Topbar

Every public surface carries one primary navigation: a sticky 48px topbar holding the wordmark, the public map (Participation, The commons, Legal, Intranet), and the mode toggle. The bar is injected by /assets/topbar.js from a single manifest, so its contents and size cannot drift page to page; the per-page inline topbars it supersedes are retired. Its geometry mirrors the shell topbar below, so crossing between the public face and a signed-in surface the frame reads as one element. Added per the U-13 packet, 2026-08-07.

Usage
<!-- in <head>, after the mode boot -->
<script src="/assets/topbar.js" defer></script>
Behaviour

Self-contained: the bar carries its own --tc-* tokens resolved from data-mode with the values on this page, so it renders identically over any page token vocabulary, old or new. The section holding the reader marks with aria-current. The bar hides in print and holds across page loads by cross-document view transitions, honoring prefers-reduced-motion. The mode toggle writes localStorage('techne-mode') and sets data-mode on the document, falling back to the device scheme on first visit. A public page loads topbar.js; a signed-in surface loads shell.js; no page loads both, and no page carries an inline topbar of its own. The validator enforces the rule: every page names exactly one of the two.

Withheld before launch

Register. As of 2026-08-10 the bar is withheld on every public page: topbar.js resolves the mode and builds nothing, behind a single switch (OFFER_MAP) in the script. The estate is still under construction, and a map is a claim about what is finished, so the steward holds the map back until the public launch on 2026-08-14. Read the lead above as the specification, not as the present state. Withholding the bar hides the map and does not close any door: every page remains public at its own address, and pages still link to each other in their own prose. Restoring the bar on launch day is one line.

Register. The front page's four-column footer is withheld with it, on the same date and for the same reason: it carried the estate's second navigation, in link columns, under the map that is already held back. Until launch the front page closes on a single band of who and where, and the withheld markup waits in place in index.html behind a comment marked for 2026-08-14. Restoring it is a comment delete.

Behaviour

Self-injects the frame on DOMContentLoaded and re-parents the page into the primary view. Reads the Supabase session from storage without creating a second auth client; pages keep their own data loading. The member chip carries the signed-in address and, once the record answers, the display name. The mode toggle writes localStorage('techne-mode') and sets data-mode on the document. The active section marks from window.location.pathname. The steward group appears only when the reader holds the steward or director role. window.cisUser is exposed and the cis:user event fires for page scripts that need member context. Signed in, the shell also declares the map's destinations to the browser as speculation rules at moderate eagerness, so the next surface is fetched on hover or pointerdown rather than after the click; browsers without the API ignore the declaration.

Register. This reference claimed from U-01 that cross-document view transitions held the topbar and map in place while the primary view changed. They did not. The opt-in lived in a stylesheet the shell injected on DOMContentLoaded, so it was absent when the browser read for it at pagereveal, and every transition on this estate was skipped from the first day to 2026-08-09, each skip rejecting with an AbortError the error boundary recorded as benign. U-18 moved the opt-in into shell.css, where it loads with the document, and the transitions now run and honor prefers-reduced-motion. The frame still re-raises itself on each navigation, so what runs is a transition of the whole view rather than the topbar and map holding still; naming them so they hold requires the frame to exist in the new document before the first paint, which is filed and not yet built.

The gate

One standard, owned by the shell (U-04): every signed-in surface presents the same card when the reader is signed out. The card carries the house label, the heading Member intranet, one sentence of instruction, a destination line read from the manifest (mark, slice, section, in the section's tint), the email field, the primary action, and a status line; after sending, the sent state with a resend affordance. The gate sends the magic link itself through the GoTrue REST seam, so no page carries a gate of its own. Signed out, the frame shows only the topbar and the gate: the map is for members. A magic-link landing, tokens in the address, renders as signing-in while the page's client completes the exchange, never as signed-out. Pages contribute nothing to authentication except loading their data once the session stands.

Layout · The Frame Measure

Every surface the shell frames uses one content measure and one alignment pattern. A new members' page adopts both and decides nothing about its own width. This section is the rule; commons.css is its emission and scripts/measure_audit.py enforces it in CI.

Why this section exists

Register. The nine framed surfaces once carried four different content widths, 720 to 1120 and one with no cap at all, and five different alignment patterns beneath them. Nothing had gone wrong; nothing had been decided. This reference named two grammars, the reading column and the widescreen instrument, and neither fitted a members' page, so each page chose for itself on the day it shipped and the estate stopped looking like one thing. Named per the U-20 packet, 2026-08-09, at the steward's direction.

The measure
/* the container of every framed surface */
<main class="wrap-frame"> ... </main>

.wrap-frame {
  width: 100%;
  max-width: 70rem;
  margin-inline: auto;
  padding-block:  clamp(var(--s5), 3.5vw, var(--s7));
  padding-inline: clamp(var(--s4), 3vw,   var(--s6));
}

Bounded, not set. The column fills a phone edge to edge, grows with the window, and stops at a comfortable maximum on a desk; the gutters scale continuously through the spacing tokens rather than jumping at a breakpoint, so there is no width on the way at which the page looks wrong. The cap is in rem, so a reader who raises their base font size gets a proportionally wider column instead of a cramped one. Prose inside the frame keeps the layer's measure at 68ch: a column wide enough for a roster is far too wide for a sentence.

The alignment pattern
/* every list of cards on a framed surface */
<div class="frame-grid"> ... </div>
<div class="frame-grid" style="--grid-min:150px"> ... </div>

.frame-grid {
  display: grid;
  grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, minmax(min(var(--grid-min, 300px), 100%), 1fr));
  gap: var(--s4);
  align-items: start;
}

A page sets the track minimum and nothing else. The grid reflows on the space it actually has, so it needs no media query and no container query.

Why intrinsic, and not a breakpoint

A framed surface sits beside a 212px map, so the viewport is not the width the content has, and a breakpoint written against the viewport fires at the wrong moment. Agreements went to two columns at a 1100px viewport while its content column measured 1056px: a rule keyed to a number the content never sees. Prefer an intrinsic grid. Where a genuine query is unavoidable, key it to a container, never to the viewport; do not put container-type on the shell's .cis-main, because the drawers are position: fixed and containment would trap them inside the content column.

Adding a page to the intranet

Give <main> the frame measure, put every card list in a frame-grid, and set --grid-min only if the default 300px is wrong for the content. Do not give main a max-width, do not write a viewport breakpoint that changes a column count, and do not introduce a second width. The measure audit fails the build on all three. The one exception in force is dense numeric tiles, which take --grid-min:150px; any other value is a design decision and belongs in a decision record rather than in a page.

Icon System

Icons are drawn from the Lucide set and inlined as SVG markup: no emoji, no icon font, no runtime library, no external request. The glyph is copied from Lucide into the page, normalized to a 24 viewBox, 1.5px stroke, round caps and joins, color inherited from currentColor. Amended per the U-08 packet, 2026-08-07: Lucide replaces the hand-drawn set below as the source of new icons; existing glyphs stand until each surface's re-emission.

<!-- a Lucide icon, inlined; decorative beside its word -->
<svg class="lucide" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor"
  stroke-width="1.5" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true">
  <path d="..."/>
</svg>

Rules. An icon is decorative beside a word, never a meaning alone: aria-hidden="true" on every inline icon, and the adjacent text carries the sense. Sizing through svg.lucide (16px inline, .lg 22px, .ico-well for card wells) in commons.css. Choose the plainest glyph that names the thing; one icon per concept, reused everywhere the concept appears.

Term Hints & Disclosure

Two instruments of gated complexity, added per U-08. A term hint gives a weighted word its definition where it stands; a disclosure holds depth behind a one-line summary, closed by default. Together they carry the cooperative principle of education, training, and information into the surface itself: the page teaches at the moment of contact, and the depth waits until asked for.

Term hint

A dotted-underline button inside prose. Hover or keyboard focus shows the definition card through CSS alone; /assets/hints.js adds tap-to-open, Escape, outside-tap close, and aria-expanded, so touch readers get the same definition. The card ends with a path deeper, usually the Lexicon. Never a title attribute: those reach neither touch nor keyboard.

Participation Cards

The class-of-participation card, added per U-08: how a newcomer meets the four classes of the Participation Framework and chooses a track. The card carries the track banner, the class number and name, the rights (a mark beside every line, the word carrying the meaning), the cost, the admission path, and one action. Cards render on public surfaces; the Participation Framework itself is drafted, unadopted, and not published, and the card cites it, never restating beyond it. Where the card is used to describe standing rather than to sell, the cost goes in .p-cost, below the rights, rather than in the .p-price head slot: price above the rights, repeated down a column of cards, reads as a column of rungs, and standing here does not follow price (P-09).

Anatomy (commons.css): .pgrid a responsive comparison grid · .pcard the card, top-ruled in its track's tint via data-tint · .p-track, .p-class, .p-name, and either .p-price or .p-cost · rights as li.yes (Lucide check, ok green) and li.no (Lucide minus, muted; the sentence states the absence) · .p-admit the admission line · .p-act the action. The member curve, the path through the classes over time, renders as .curve steps: when, what, one sentence.

Forms & States

The v4 form and the three honest states, added per U-08. Every form is a real <form> whose submit is the form's own event; every label is bound to its control with for; every hint sits under its field; the status line carries aria-live="polite" so a screen reader hears what a sighted member sees.

Form anatomy: .field > label (mono, uppercase, .req mark) + control (serif, 2px radius, blue focus border) + .field-hint (mono, muted, never --faint) · .form-status with .ok / .err, present in the DOM from first paint so the live region exists before the first message.

States: loading is .skel, a quiet shimmer that respects reduced motion, never a spinner; absence is .absence, a sentence naming why the thing is not here and what it waits on, after the Share door's practice; a member's first visit to a surface may show one .first-run orientation card, dismissable, its dismissal kept on the device, never shown again.

Content Standards

No emoji. Emoji are not permitted anywhere on techne.coop, in body copy, headings, navigation, badges, avatars, status indicators, or interactive controls. This is an absolute rule with no exceptions.

Use typographic and geometric Unicode instead: arrows (→ ← ↑ ↓), geometric shapes (◈ ⬡ ⊗ △ ◇), mathematical operators (× ±), dashes, and ellipses (…). These carry semantic weight without decorative distraction and render consistently across environments.

Do not use
Use instead
Status emoji (check, cross, warning marks)
Status colors with text labels
Pictographic avatar emoji (faces, robots, symbols)
Initials, geometric glyphs (◈ ⬡ △)
UI affordance emoji (link, clipboard, magnifier)
SVG icons or text labels
Emoji-variant arrow (U+27A1)
Text arrow → (U+2192)

techne.coop is a professional coordination surface. Emoji carry platform-specific rendering, undefined semantic meaning, and decorative noise inconsistent with the studio's precision. Typography and geometry are the visual language here. This reference follows the rule it states: the forbidden categories are named in words rather than rendered.
