The ways in

Techne is owned and governed by the people who use it, and there is more than one way to use it. One line runs through everything below: on one side of it you are a member of RegenHub, LCA, with the governance and financial rights the statute attaches to that word, and on the other side you are a participant, which is a way of belonging here that is not ownership. Where you sit is not a function of what you pay.

The shape of it

The founding record drew belonging as concentric rather than stacked: a core of members, then people who use the space without joining, then people who come for an evening, then the wider field of people who might one day. That is still the picture. The membership line is one cut drawn across those rings, and it decides exactly one thing, which is whether you own part of the cooperative.

  1. The core. Cooperative Members, and Investor Members beside them. One vote each, and only here.
  2. At a desk or in the room. Co-working and Community Participants. Here every day, and not owners.
  3. In the guild, from anywhere. Guild Participants, when the guild opens.
  4. The field. Everyone who comes to a gathering, reads the record, or has not arrived yet. Nothing is asked of them.

The dashed line is the legal boundary, and it crosses the rings rather than ringing them. A person outside it can be closer to this cooperative than a person inside it. What the line governs is ownership, not standing among people.

Investor Member · Class Four

An Investor Member contributes capital and receives allocated returns and a Schedule K-1, and is a member of the cooperative in the statutory sense. What they do not receive is a vote. This is not a step above or below anything on the tracks: it is a stake rather than a voice, which is a different question from belonging and is drawn here on its own axis for that reason. Control follows people, not capital. One member, one vote is the cooperative's first rule, and the investor class is built to honor it.

Direct investment, not a progression from another class. No dues and no membership fee. Terms set by the Board under an Investor Agreement that is not yet drafted. Write to the board.

Crossing the line

Membership is not earned by accumulation and it is not awarded by a threshold being reached. It begins when a member who knows your work says so, and it completes when you agree. Both halves are acts of people.

How participation becomes ownership the two pathways

At a desk: a Co-working Participant who reaches a board-established patronage threshold may be invited to apply for Cooperative Member status. On Board acceptance, the applicant executes the Membership Agreement, pays the $100 one-time share, and moves to the patron track. The credits earned at a desk inform the invitation; the threshold and the credit methodology are set by board resolution and specified in Schedule A of the Participation Framework, which is drafted and not published.

Anywhere on the access track: after ninety days of continuous participation in good standing, a participant becomes invitable. When two Cooperative Members have each recorded an invitation in the cooperative's record, the participant is eligible to apply. An invitation is a member's act, not a form's checkbox: it says a member knows this person's work and direction of travel. Board acceptance, the Membership Agreement, and the $100 share follow exactly as above. Eligibility opens the door and never pushes anyone through it; membership is chosen from both sides.

Nothing on this site will show you a score, a progress bar, or a percentage toward membership, and that is a design decision rather than an omission. A figure a person watches becomes a figure a person plays. What can be shown is what happened: days established, invitations recorded, and what remains, read from the record and never compared against anybody else.

Why the classes are shaped this way

The reasoning is not on this page. It is in The Commonplace, the record of the fifteen meetings between August 2025 and February 2026 in which this cooperative was worked out: the decision that member classes should be a matrix and not a ladder, the picture of belonging as concentric rings, and the moment the room chose trust over reputation and symbols over scores. It also holds the sentence that this page is an answer to, from the November 2025 consultation with counsel: whether calling these levels of participation rather than distinct roles avoids the problem or postpones it was left an open question.

The Commonplace binds nothing and confers nothing. It is a record of what was said, held as a lens rather than a rule, and where it and an instrument disagree the instrument is right.
